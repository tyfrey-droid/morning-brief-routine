🌍 World
  • US and Japan prop up the yen — Tokyo and Washington confirmed their first coordinated currency intervention in 15 years to halt the yen's slide from four-decade lows, driving the dollar down about 1% to roughly 156 yen after it had topped 163. Japan's finance minister Satsuki Katayama said the action, backed by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, was meant to counter "excessive volatility"; President Trump said the US stepped in because Japan "wanted a little bit of help." (NPR, CNBC)
  • Deadly ferry fire off Indonesia — The passenger ferry Mutiara Sentosa 2, sailing from Surabaya to Makassar with hundreds aboard plus 181 vehicles, caught fire off Madura island Sunday; at least five people are dead and dozens missing after roughly 229 were rescued. Officials said more passengers were aboard than the manifest listed, leaving the number unaccounted for unclear, and a survivor said the blaze may have started with a truck on the vehicle deck. (NPR, Al Jazeera)
  • Nazca Lines tourist plane crashes — A small Aerodiana sightseeing plane crashed in Peru's Ica region while flying tourists over the Nazca Lines, killing all 13 aboard, including seven Italians, two Germans and two Spaniards plus two crew. The pilot had reported a mechanical problem before radio contact was lost, and Peru's transport ministry ordered the airline to suspend operations pending an investigation. (CNN, NBC News)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • Trump weighs firing US Attorney Pirro — DC US Attorney Jeanine Pirro was summoned to the White House on Monday as Trump privately floated ousting her — a sharp escalation after he said she "choked" and "folded like an umbrella" by dropping charges over damage to the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool. Pirro's office had withdrawn the case, citing National Park Service communications indicating the damage was hard to attribute to deliberate vandalism, contradicting Trump's insistence it was intentional. (The Hill, Forbes)
  • Clayton sworn in as intelligence chief — Jay Clayton, the former SEC chairman and US Attorney for the Southern District of New York, was sworn in Monday as Director of National Intelligence after a party-line 51-47 Senate vote, filling the post Tulsi Gabbard vacated in June. His confirmation drew Democratic opposition, in part after he declined at his hearing to directly acknowledge that Trump lost the 2020 election. (CNBC, NPR)
⚡ Tech & AI
  • Apple to link iPhone and Windows clipboards — Apple confirmed it is building cross-device clipboard sharing so users can copy on an iPhone and paste on a Windows PC, a response to a Microsoft interoperability request under the EU's Digital Markets Act. Apple called it a significant engineering effort built on its Accessory Transport Extension framework and said it won't ship until around fall 2027, arriving in the EU first. (9to5Mac, MacRumors)
  • OpenAI fires back at Apple suit — OpenAI publicly rebutted Apple's July trade-secrets lawsuit, calling the allegations "careless, aggressive and oddly personal" and publishing iMessage and email exchanges as evidence. OpenAI argued that Apple staff had themselves contacted the departed employees for help, and noted that Apple's lawyers "emailed the wrong person after confusing two Asian last names." (9to5Mac, Forbes)
  • Apple caps AI-spam bug reports — Facing a deluge of low-quality, AI-generated vulnerability reports, Apple imposed a submission quota and a 30-day cool-off period on its security portal, per the Financial Times. Legitimate researchers can request higher quotas, and Apple has deployed its own AI to help triage incoming reports. (9to5Mac)
  • Telegram briefly pulled from App Store — Apple removed Telegram from all 175 App Store storefronts on Aug. 3 after its review process found child sexual abuse material accessible in the app. The app was restored hours later once Telegram removed the content and banned the user; existing installs kept working throughout. (9to5Mac)
  • Palantir soars on 93% growth — Palantir reported second-quarter revenue up 93% to about $1.9 billion, with US commercial sales up 149%, beating estimates and lifting full-year guidance above $8.1 billion and sending shares up double digits. In his shareholder letter, CEO Alex Karp branded frontier AI labs "Marxist" and too untrustworthy for serious enterprise deployment. (TechCrunch, CNBC)
  • DeepSeek's V4-Flash undercuts rivals — Research firm Artificial Analysis found DeepSeek's newly released V4-Flash is the cheapest well-known AI model to run — roughly three cents per benchmark test, at $0.14 and $0.28 per million input and output tokens. The 284-billion-parameter model undercuts GPT-5.4 Nano, Gemini 3.1 Flash and Claude Haiku 4.5, though it still trails top frontier models on knowledge tests. (The Next Web)

🌊 San Diego
  • F-35B jet crashes at Miramar — A single-seat F-35B Lightning II went down short of the runway and burst into flames at Marine Corps Air Station Miramar just before 10 a.m. Friday; the pilot ejected and was hospitalized in stable condition with non-life-threatening injuries. The Marines classified it a "Class A mishap," their most severe category, and investigators are still working to determine the cause. (NBC 7 San Diego)
  • San Diego braces for El Niño winter — Local agencies are preparing for a winter of weather extremes as a strong El Niño builds, warning of possible canyon and hillside floods, large surf that could carve away beaches and pound piers, or the opposite scenario of sparse rain and elevated fire risk. Officials are staging flood-control and emergency plans for whichever way it breaks. (KPBS)
  • La Mesa moves to raise cannabis tax — The La Mesa City Council voted unanimously to direct staff to draft an increase in the city's retail cannabis tax from 4% to 5%, a hike that could bring in roughly $750,000 more a year for the general fund. La Mesa currently has the county's lowest cannabis rate; the change would match Lemon Grove and Oceanside. (Times of San Diego)
  • Grossmont-Cuyamaca bond heads to ballot — The Grossmont-Cuyamaca Community College District board voted unanimously to place a measure on the Nov. 3 ballot extending 2002's Proposition R, generating an estimated $624 million for campus repairs and modernization without raising the current tax rate. Planned projects include wildfire-protection upgrades, new career-technical trades facilities and a rebuilt child development center. (East County Magazine)
  • Hand warmers tied to local death recalled — Federal regulators announced a recall of about 1.5 million OCOOPA rechargeable hand warmers after their lithium-ion batteries were found to overheat and ignite, citing 15 fires, 350 burn injuries and the February death of an 83-year-old San Diego resident. Owners of the affected models should stop using them immediately and seek a refund. (NBC 7 San Diego)
