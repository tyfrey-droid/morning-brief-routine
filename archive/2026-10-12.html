🧬 Mito Research
  • Two approved drugs clear a protein jam in mitochondrial disease — Not MELAS, and worth saying so plainly: this work is on POLGA, the catalytic subunit of the enzyme that copies mitochondrial DNA, whose mutations cause progressive external ophthalmoplegia. The mechanism is new. A team reports in EMBO Molecular Medicine that a mitochondrial enzyme called MITOL over-tags mutant POLGA at a single lysine, which bends the protein into insoluble clumps that never make it into the mitochondrion — so mtDNA replication fails. Screening a library of drugs already approved by the FDA, EMA and Japan’s regulator, they found two — diltiazem, a common blood-pressure calcium-channel blocker, and the antibiotic sulbactam — that broke up the aggregates, restored import and mtDNA replication, and measurably improved motor performance in mice carrying the disease-equivalent mutation. Why it matters here: it is preclinical and gene-specific, so nothing changes for a child with m.3243A>G today, but it establishes protein aggregation as a treatable failure mode in a primary mitochondrial disease and does it with drugs that already have human safety records — the fastest available path from a mouse to a clinic. (PubMed, EMBO Molecular Medicine)
