🌍 World

  • Pope Leo has lung cancer — Surgeons at Rome's Gemelli Hospital removed the lower lobe of the pope's right lung on Tuesday using a video-assisted technique, and an examination of the tissue during the operation confirmed lung adenocarcinoma — the diagnosis his pre-operative tests had suspected. The Vatican said the roughly 90-minute procedure went without complications and that the American pope, who had no previously known health problems and is an active tennis player and swimmer, passed a calm first night; he is expected to stay about a week for further tests on the removed tissue. His audiences are cancelled until October 20, and a Latin America tour is on the calendar for November 6–17. (Vatican News, PBS NewsHour)
  • Kyiv loses power under a ballistic barrage — Russia fired 39 ballistic missiles and more than 120 drones at Ukraine overnight Monday into Tuesday, hitting Kyiv's CHP-4 power plant and a nine-story apartment building and leaving roughly 600,000 people without electricity. At least three people were killed and more than 20 injured, with counts still being revised. Volodymyr Zelensky said the ballistic launches came with no advance warning from Ukraine's Western partners, and Ukrainian officials said intelligence-sharing had been interrupted. (RFE/RL, Kyiv Independent)

🇺🇸 US & Politics

  • The ambassador to Greece is out — The White House asked Kimberly Guilfoyle to resign on Tuesday, and she confirmed she is leaving. New press secretary Katie Zacharia broke the news in her first briefing — "She has been a wonderful asset to the team, and we wish her well" — and the president said only that "she was doing a good job, but we didn't like what we were reading." The reporting that preceded it included text messages indicating she asked an associate for $100,000 toward an American Express bill before her Senate confirmation, and she had been under review by the State Department's inspector general. Guilfoyle calls the allegations false and says she will defend her reputation; none has been adjudicated. (NPR, NBC News, CBS News)
  • Agents search Ilhan Omar's home — Homeland Security Investigations executed what the Justice Department called a judicially authorized search warrant at the Minnesota congresswoman's Washington-area home on Monday, part of what NBC describes as a yearslong inquiry run with the U.S. attorney in Minneapolis. The New York Times reported the warrant listed potential immigration-related offenses including false statements and fraudulent paperwork; investigators have also subpoenaed relatives. Omar has not been charged and calls the search an attempt to "weaponize federal law enforcement." She is on the ballot in three weeks, which is why Democratic leaders have pointed to the department's longstanding practice of avoiding overt steps against candidates close to an election. (PBS NewsHour, NBC News, Axios)
  • Twelve senators move against the diesel deal — Monday's brief carried the Putin diesel arrangement as an announcement; it now has a bipartisan revolt attached. Six Democrats and six Republicans — Blumenthal, Coons, Durbin, Reed, Shaheen and Whitehouse alongside Armstrong, Collins, Cornyn, McConnell, Murkowski and Tillis — signed a letter urging the president to rescind it and instead use the Russia sanctions Congress authorized last month. They argue letting Russian-origin diesel into global markets offers Americans "limited to no relief" while funding the war, and that the administration skipped a statutory consultation with Congress. Tillis went further on Sunday, saying there is "a legitimate case" that the deal is illegal. (NPR, Axios)

⚡ Tech & AI

  • Apple's home push finally has prices — The "Welcome home" event in Tribeca on Tuesday turned two weeks of Gurman reporting into a shipping lineup. HomeView, Apple's first smart display, is $299 with a 7-inch Liquid Retina screen on a speaker base or a wall mount; it uses Apple Intelligence to recognise up to six household members and ships November 13, with pre-orders open now and no EU launch yet while Apple works through regulators.   • Gemini 4 Argon opens to everyone — When Google unveiled Argon on September 30 the catch was access: it went only to a small set of cyber defenders, which is where our October 2 brief left it. As of Tuesday it is global — in the API through AI Studio, on Google Cloud, and in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search for Pro and Ultra subscribers. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with cached input discounted 95%; both rates double after November 30. It supports a one-million-token unified context window, so a single response can run to a million tokens. (Google, Digital Trends)

🌊 San Diego

  • Flu is early, and worse than usual — County surveillance has recorded eight influenza deaths already, four times the count at this point in each of the last three years, alongside 58 hospitalizations and 8% test positivity — and San Diego's season normally does not pick up until early November. All eight who died were over 50 and six were unvaccinated. H1N1 is the dominant strain and is covered by this year's vaccine. A new forecasting tool built with UC San Diego's Resilient Shield program projects hospitalizations climbing over the next fortnight; the county's public health officer put it plainly: it will get worse before it gets better. (KPBS)
  • The city can now sue over stolen wages — The City Council voted unanimously Tuesday to amend the municipal code so San Diego can bring civil actions to recover unpaid wages and overtime, authority the previous language did not grant. Councilmembers Marni von Wilpert and Sean Elo-Rivera brought the changes forward. (KPBS)
  • An immigration surge starts here — Reporting on a federal enforcement push described as Operation Safe California has it beginning in San Diego, and local advocacy groups spent Monday organising responses and know-your-rights outreach. One man was apparently detained in Mira Mesa on Monday morning on his way to work. (KPBS)

🎥 Filmmaking Gear

  • A $99 autofocus pancake for E-mount — TTArtisan announced the AF 24mm f/3.8 Mini on Tuesday, a full-frame Sony E lens that weighs 57 grams and costs $99, with a launch promotion running to October 30. The compromises are real and worth knowing up front: no weather sealing, no filter thread, and no manual focus ring at all. (Newsshooter, Photo Rumors)

Tilta still has not said whether the XLR handle can run at the same time, which is the question if you work with on-board audio. Reported pricing has been inconsistent across Tilta's own listings, so check before ordering. (Newsshooter, CineD)

The odd coda: it has climbed to number one on Amazon's new-release list for professional video cameras while being impossible to buy, and Sony's own window has drifted from an August target to late October "or later."
