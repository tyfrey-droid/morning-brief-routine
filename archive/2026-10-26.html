  • Lula wins a fourth term in Brazil — Friday's brief had Sunday's runoff as a statistical tie. The count gave President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva roughly 51.8 per cent of valid votes to Senator Flávio Bolsonaro's 48.2, with nearly all ballots in — the first time since direct elections returned in 1989 that a candidate has won the runoff after losing the first round. The movement grew out of the protests that followed the 2024 collapse of a railway-station canopy in Novi Sad, which killed 16 people. 🇺🇸 US & Politics

  • The Supreme Court lets New Hampshire bar student IDs — An unsigned emergency order on October 22 cleared the state to enforce its ban on student identification at the polls for the midterms, granting a Republican-backed appeal after a district judge had blocked the secretary of state's guidance in early October. The majority leaned on the Purcell principle, under which federal courts avoid changing election rules close to an election, and said the lower court pointed to nothing justifying a departure from it. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented alone, arguing the blocked order concerned accurate information rather than a late rule change. It is not a ruling on the merits and the case can return after the election. New Hampshire has one of the closest Senate races on the board. (PBS NewsHour, NBC News, The Hill)
  • The DOJ sues to pre-block an Alaska Senate result — The Justice Department asked a court on October 22 to bar Alaska from certifying its November 3 Senate race if Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan loses. The suit turns on a second Republican-listed candidate with nearly the same name, Daniel J. Sullivan, whom the department argues could siphon or misdirect votes under ranked-choice voting and hand the seat to Democrat Mary Peltola. Trump said he ordered the filing. Critics note a voter can simply rank both Sullivans first and second, and Alaska's supreme court had already rejected a challenge to the second Sullivan's place on the ballot. Peltola's campaign and Senate Democrats have moved to dismiss, calling it a pre-emptive strike on an election; legal scholars quoted expect a court to wait until after the vote. (CNN, CBS News, NBC News, Alaska Beacon)
  • The closing week runs through red states — Eight days out from the November 3 midterms, Trump's final itinerary is built around seats his party already holds: Mississippi on Monday for Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith, whose race against Democrat Scott Colom has tightened, then Texas, Ohio, Florida and Iowa. (CNN, The Hill)
