🧬 Mito Research
  • The metformin question gets real data — Diabetes is common in mitochondrial disease, and clinicians have long been cautious about metformin in m.3243A>G carriers on theoretical grounds — the drug inhibits complex I and carries a lactic-acidosis warning. A multinational analysis of the GENOMIT registry (2,399 patients across German, Austrian and Italian sites) published Sept. 1 in EBioMedicine now puts numbers to both halves of that. Risk is sharply genotype-specific: 49% of m.3243A>G carriers developed diabetes, a hazard ratio of 10.3 versus other mitochondrial genotypes, and it was the only variant significantly associated with it — while sex, BMI, smoking and hypertension were not. Median onset was 47.7 years. Among 281 patients with mitochondrial diabetes, metformin was the most-used non-insulin drug and was discontinued in only 8 of 50 users. The authors’ conclusion is the useful part: while caution is warranted, the data do not justify universally avoiding metformin in this population. That is a narrow finding, and it concerns an adult-onset complication rather than childhood disease — but it is a named drug, a specific genotype, and real-world evidence where there had been only mechanism. (PubMed, doi.org)
