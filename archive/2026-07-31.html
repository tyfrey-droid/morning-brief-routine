🌍 World
  • Mass crossing overwhelms Ceuta — Tens of thousands of migrants crossed from Morocco into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta in roughly 24 hours — a figure equal to a large share of the small city's population — with authorities reporting at least 18 deaths, many by drowning. Spain deployed the military to support the Civil Guard, and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez traveled to Ceuta, calling the breach a violation of Spain's territorial integrity as officials described an unfolding humanitarian crisis. (NPR, Washington Post, CNN)
  • Trump touts Hamas disarmament deal — President Trump said his "Board of Peace," working with Egypt, Qatar and Turkey, reached an agreement for the "complete disarmament" of Hamas and other armed groups in Gaza, with a roadmap to be drafted within two weeks and weapons surrendered in phases over several months alongside Israeli withdrawals. Neither Hamas nor Israel confirmed the terms, and analysts and a senior Hamas official flagged major unresolved questions over sequencing. (NPR, Axios, Al Jazeera)
  • Iran conflict draws in Egypt — The US–Iran conflict widened as Cairo confirmed a drone struck two vessels at the Mediterranean port of Damietta, the first reported strike on Egyptian soil since the war began — no party claimed responsibility and no casualties were reported. US forces continued strikes on Iranian military and intelligence targets, while Iran-linked fire hit a building in Kuwait, killing one worker, and Jordan said it intercepted five missiles. (CNN, Washington Times)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • Senate blocks Iran war-powers measure — The Senate voted 50–49 against advancing a war-powers resolution that would have required President Trump to withdraw US forces from the Iran conflict without congressional authorization — the second such measure to fail in two weeks. Republicans Rand Paul, Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski crossed over to support it, while Democrat John Fetterman voted no. (Washington Post, The Hill, Roll Call)
  • US growth slows in second quarter — The Commerce Department reported the economy grew at a 1.5% annualized rate in the second quarter, down from 2.1% and below forecasts, with economists pointing to tariffs, a wider trade deficit, and higher oil prices tied to the Iran war. Resilient consumer spending and AI-related business investment were bright spots; June core inflation ran at 3.3%. (CNBC, CBS News, CNN)

The deal reflects "neocloud" providers racing to own more of the AI compute stack beyond raw hardware. (TechCrunch, SiliconANGLE)
  • Oracle expands Google Gemini deal — Oracle shares jumped more than 8% after it said it would widen its Google Cloud partnership to bring Gemini AI models to business customers across its applications and cloud infrastructure, deepening a multi-model strategy for enterprise deployments. The plan bundles AppleCare+ benefits including accidental-damage repairs and theft-and-loss coverage. (MacRumors, AppleInsider)

🌊 San Diego
  • AI cameras coming to Coronado Bridge — Caltrans is moving ahead on a roughly $145 million project to add an 8-foot steel-wire suicide-prevention barrier along the full length of the San Diego–Coronado Bridge and replace its cameras with AI-powered units that flag unusual activity and alert the CHP. Construction is expected to begin this fall and finish by 2028 on what is among the nation's deadliest bridges. (Voice of San Diego)
  • County rent-cap ceiling drops to 8.2% — Starting Aug. 1, the maximum allowable annual rent increase in the San Diego region falls to 8.2%, down from 8.8% — among the lowest caps in years — after slowing local inflation lowered the federal cost-of-living adjustment. The cap holds until the next adjustment in August 2027. (inewsource)
  • Extreme-heat warning through the weekend — An extreme-heat warning and expanded advisories cover much of the county from Friday through Sunday, with inland valleys in the mid-90s and foothills near 105. Officials urged residents to hydrate, check on neighbors, and limit midday exertion. (Times of San Diego)
