🌍 World
  • Ukraine's largest drone assault — Ukraine sent hundreds of drones deep into Russia overnight Saturday into Sunday, killing at least six people in what Russian state media called the biggest such attack since the start of the year. Russia's defence ministry said it downed 822 drones; Moscow's mayor reported roughly 600 headed toward the capital region, where an 83-year-old man was killed in his home, while five died in the southwestern Rostov region and a Wildberries warehouse burned in Podolsk. A Russian missile strike on Kryvyi Rih killed two and wounded 14, Zelenskyy said. (NPR, CNN, CBS News)
  • Kushner meets Hamas in Egypt — Jared Kushner held a rare face-to-face meeting Sunday in Egypt with Hamas political leader Khalil al-Hayya, alongside Egypt's intelligence chief and senior Qatari and Turkish officials, in an effort to restart the stalled 15-point US road map. Hamas reaffirmed a commitment to disarm and demilitarize Gaza in the roughly 90-minute session; Kushner travels on to Israel for talks with Netanyahu, who last week called the framework unacceptable. (NPR, Axios, Al Jazeera)
  • Bus crash in Hungary kills 12 — A coach carrying Polish pilgrims home from Bosnia-Herzegovina left the M3 motorway about 87 miles east of Budapest near Mezőkeresztes around 1 a.m. Sunday and overturned in a ditch, killing 12 and seriously injuring at least 10. Rescuers pulled trapped passengers out through the windows and used two cranes to lift the bus; police said the driver likely fell asleep and has been taken into custody. (ABC News, Washington Post, RTÉ)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • Indiana floods break a 1913 record — At least seven people have died as historic flooding continues across eastern and central Indiana, where more than 11 inches of rain fell in three days beginning Aug. 11 behind a derecho that cut power to over a million customers. The White River topped its 1913 crest at both Anderson (24.9 ft) and Noblesville, 15 miles upstream of Indianapolis; Delaware County reported more than 350 evacuations, and Trump approved an emergency declaration Saturday opening FEMA assistance. (NPR, CBS News)
  • Lala batters Hawaii's Big Island — Lala weakened to a tropical storm Sunday after its eyewall skirted Hawaii Island without making landfall, but not before hurricane-force gusts and extraordinary rain — close to three feet in places, and as much as 35 inches on the slopes of Mauna Kea. One person died in a storm-related crash and 100 or more homes were damaged or destroyed; flash floods swept occupied houses off their foundations in Naʻalehu and Waiʻohinu, prompting a search-and-rescue effort involving the National Guard and a Coast Guard helicopter. (NBC News, CNN, PBS NewsHour)

🌊 San Diego
  • Grand jury faults the bike network — A county civil grand jury report, "Shifting Gears," finds San Diego's roughly 1,900 miles of bikeways are dominated by painted Class II lanes, with only about 64 miles of protected Class IV cycle tracks that are often narrow, chopped into short segments and clogged with gravel and debris — the city runs just one operable sweeper configured for them. The panel flags corridors east of 28th Street, MLK Jr. Way, Imperial Avenue and Ocean View Boulevard as low-income areas without continuous routes, and notes only about one in 20 San Diego Unified campuses has an active Safe Routes to School program. (Times of San Diego, OB Rag)
  • College-Rolando library loses parking — Construction has begun on a five-story housing project next to the College-Rolando branch library, and Friends of the Library worry the loss of adjacent parking will keep patrons away from a branch that functions as a neighborhood hub. The permit covers 125 extended-stay rental units and 57 apartments; the developer plans 160 resident-only indoor spaces plus 25 outdoor spaces library visitors may use, but neighbors expect shared driveways onto Montezuma Road and Mohawk Street to squeeze access. A 2016 fencing dispute cut visits until a joint-use deal restored parking. (Times of San Diego)

🧬 Mito Research
  • A lamotrigine caution in mito epilepsy — Neurologists at Hebei Medical University report a MELAS patient who developed fatal Stevens-Johnson syndrome more than a month after starting lamotrigine for epilepsy, and argue that the immune-activating environment created by mitochondrial dysfunction may itself raise the risk of this severe hypersensitivity reaction. Their recommendation is caution and closer monitoring when starting lamotrigine in mitochondrial encephalomyopathy — a drug widely used for exactly this population. Worth flagging: this is a single case report in a 67-year-old, so it is hypothesis-generating rather than established risk, and a question for a treating neurologist rather than a reason to change anything unilaterally. (Ther Adv Neurol Disord, via PubMed · DOI)
