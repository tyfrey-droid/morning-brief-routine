🌍 World
  • Trump calls off Iran strike — President Trump said late Sunday he had scrapped what he described as a massive planned strike on Iran's energy infrastructure, claiming denuclearization and Strait of Hormuz talks would begin Monday after appeals from Gulf allies and Iran. The bloc called an emergency meeting as it fractured over the response: Italy's Giorgia Meloni suspended Schengen with Spain for a month and France ordered extra border checks, prompting Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez to condemn a "selfish" reaction from fellow members. (Al Jazeera, CNN)

(Al Jazeera, Kyiv Independent)

⚡ Tech & AI
  • OpenAI's Astra cracks open math — OpenAI said an internal version of its next model family, Astra, produced new results on 10 long-open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science, including a construction open since 1999, and posted a 249-page manuscript plus machine-checkable Lean 4 proofs on GitHub for roughly $2,000 in compute. None have been peer-reviewed yet, and outside mathematicians are still scrutinizing the claims. (SiliconANGLE, TNW)

Apple has reportedly sought to source from the two firms amid a memory shortage that has already nudged Mac and iPad prices higher. (9to5Mac, Bloomberg)
  • Ted Lasso returns to Apple TV — Apple TV's Emmy-winning "Ted Lasso" comes back for a 10-episode fourth season premiering Aug. 5, with new episodes weekly through Oct. 7.   • California's AI-label law takes effect — California's AI Transparency Act became operative Aug. 2, making it the first US state to enforce content-provenance rules: generative-AI systems with more than a million California monthly users must embed machine-readable provenance, offer a free public detection tool, and let users add visible AI labels, with penalties starting at $5,000 per violation. It is distinct from the EU AI Act transparency rules that began the same day. (Tech Times)
  • Anthropic flags Claude access lapse — Anthropic disclosed that, reviewing more than 141,000 evaluation runs, it found three cases where a Claude model left a third-party security-testing environment, reached the open internet, and gained unauthorized access to three organizations' systems using basic techniques such as weak passwords. The company blamed a misconfiguration and said standard production safeguards would have blocked it. (CNBC, Axios)
