🇺🇸 US & Politics

  • Democrats land their biggest House majority since 1992 — The final uncalled competitive race broke over the weekend when Republican Virginia Foxx conceded a North Carolina seat decided by roughly 330 votes, putting Democrats at 258 seats to Republicans' 173 and confirming the party's largest House majority in 34 years. A handful of races remain uncalled, and analysts expect the final count to land around 260. The practical ceiling is still the Senate and the White House, so the majority buys subpoena power, committee control and the floor agenda rather than a path to enacting legislation. (Al Jazeera, NBC News, The Hill)
  • An appeals court voids the pregnant workers law — The Fifth Circuit ruled 9–8 on Friday that the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act was not validly enacted, because the House passed it in 2022 with members voting by proxy under pandemic rules rather than being physically present for a quorum. The decision reaches Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi and does not take effect for several weeks, leaving room for an appeal; A Better Balance, which campaigned for the law, says it will go to the Supreme Court. The law requires employers to make reasonable accommodations such as schedule changes or lighter duties for pregnancy and childbirth. (WLWT, WRTV)
  • Thanksgiving travel dips for the first time in four years — AAA expects 81.5 million Americans to travel at least 50 miles between Tuesday and the following Monday, down a fraction from last year's 81.6 million and ending a three-year run of records, which AAA attributes to cost rather than appetite. Driving still accounts for 72.9 million of those trips; domestic round-trip airfares are averaging about $800, up 17 per cent on AAA's booking data. Atlanta and Dallas-Fort Worth are the busiest airports, and AAA does not expect this month's FAA router failure to affect the holiday. (AAA, ABC News)

  • The AI trade drags the market into a correction — The S&P 500 fell about 3.7 per cent on Friday, its seventh consecutive decline and worst streak since February 2020, pulling both it and the Dow into correction territory while the Nasdaq erased its gains for 2026. Nvidia did most of the damage, dropping roughly 12 to 14 per cent to an 18-month low and leaving it down about 47 per cent from its October 6 peak, as investors picked over the $102 billion in data-centre lease and cloud commitments disclosed in its quarterly report and the fallout from Jensen Huang's departure.   • Siri's AI requests get a daily cap tied to iCloud+ — The second developer beta of iOS 27.3 introduces daily usage limits on the heavier Siri features, and the size of your allowance scales with your iCloud+ tier. What counts: multi-step requests that use personal context, Image Playground, Clean Up, Extend and Reframe in Photos, and cloud model calls made through Shortcuts or third-party apps using Private Cloud Compute. What does not: timers, alarms, messages and the other on-device basics. Limits are per device and reset at local midnight. Apple has not published the actual numbers and this is still beta behaviour, so treat the tier multipliers circulating in coverage as provisional. (Macworld, The Mac Observer)
  • Apple closed its support forums on Friday — The Apple Support Community, running in one form or another for roughly 28 years, shut permanently on November 20; the discussions site now redirects to Apple's main support page. Apple told MacRumors it decided to sunset the forums “after a careful evaluation of strategy and customer needs,” and has not said what becomes of the archive, though MacRumors reports existing posts will eventually be deleted. If you have ever solved a Mac problem by landing on a decade-old thread there, that path is closing; Apple is steering people to direct support instead. (MacRumors, MacRumors)

🌊 San Diego

  • King tides run through Thanksgiving — The city is bracing for a run of extremely high tides starting today and continuing through Thursday, with the National Weather Service projecting peaks of roughly 7.9 to 8.2 feet from Tuesday to Thanksgiving Day. Crews have been reinforcing sand berms and the city is making sandbags available; low-lying coastal spots and the usual flood-prone streets are the exposure. The city council already passed an emergency ban on new data centres, but it cannot reach federal property, which is the gap the bill is meant to close. (KPBS)
  • Officials keep pressing for local control of robotaxis — Council member Sean Elo-Rivera, who chairs the city's Taxi Advisory Committee, is lobbying state lawmakers to hand cities more authority over driverless vehicles, which are currently regulated almost entirely at the state level through the DMV and the Public Utilities Commission. San Diego has no mechanism of its own to set conditions on where or how robotaxis operate. (KPBS)

🎥 Filmmaking Gear

  • Libec shows a quick-deploy tripod pair — Newsshooter’s Inter BEE first look covers Libec’s Q30B and Q50B, two-stage tripods built around a single ring on the spreader that opens the legs and locks the spreader in one motion. Both carry a 45kg payload at roughly 2.9–3.0kg, which is the interesting part — the Q30B takes a 75mm ball and the Q50B a 100mm, so either one will sit under a built-up FX6 without being the heaviest thing in the bag. Libec says the locks are dust-resistant and the legs were field-tested for over a year; show pricing was around 80,000 yen and 132,000 yen, and US figures were not set. (Newsshooter, CineD)
  • NANLITE’s vela spotlights reach production — The prototypes NANLITE showed at IBC turned up at Inter BEE as four shipping fixtures: the bicolour vela 150b and 300b and the full-colour 150c and 300c, all Bowens-mount with the power supply built into the head, so they run off an AC cable with no separate ballast to find a place for. The 150s use a 21–40° Fresnel and the 300s a 40° reflector, with on-board or NANLINK app control. List prices run $209 / $249 / $299 / $349, discounted for Black Friday; regional availability varies, with some markets not getting the 300s until the new year. (Newsshooter, RedShark News)
  • Blackmagic's ambisonic ball goes on sale next month — Blackmagic Design announced a 7th-order ambisonic microphone built from 108 MEMS capsules in a sphere, recording spatial audio over a single USB-C cable and shipping in December. It is Apple-licensed and aimed squarely at immersive production for Vision Pro, and it writes about 33GB an hour, so plan storage accordingly. Note the price is unsettled in coverage: Blackmagic's US store lists $1,909, while announcement-day reporting quoted $1,695, with a $2,405 kit adding a windshield and a locking cable. (PetaPixel, Videomaker)

🔋 F-150 Lightning & EVs

  • Ford's first US-made LFP cells head for a $28,350 pickup — Ford has started producing lithium-iron-phosphate battery cells in the United States using licensed CATL technology, and the first of them are earmarked for the Fathom, the compact electric pickup Ford has priced from $28,350 and expects to bring to market near the end of 2027. LFP chemistry is cheaper and longer-cycling than the nickel-based packs in the current electric F-150, at the cost of cold-weather performance and energy density. Planned cell capacity has been scaled back from Ford's earlier targets, and Bill Ford says the truck will be exported rather than sold only domestically. (Electrek, CNBC, Ford Authority)
