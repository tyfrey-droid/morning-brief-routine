🧬 Mito Research

  • Ketogenic therapy looks genotype-dependent in mitochondrial disease — A review in Nutrients from two pediatric neurologists at Yonsei University College of Medicine pulls together the evidence on ketogenic dietary therapies and β-hydroxybutyrate in mitochondrial function, and the clinically useful part is the split it draws: benefit in primary mitochondrial disease appears genotype-dependent, with potential benefit for mtDNA point-variant carriers but possible harm in POLG-related disease and mtDNA deletion myopathy. The authors are candid about the limits — most clinical studies never measured mitochondrial outcomes directly, ketogenic feeding suppresses mitochondrial one-carbon metabolism, and some signalling effects only appear at ketone levels above what diet alone achieves — and call for genotype-stratified trials. This is a synthesis of existing data, not new trial results, but for an mtDNA point mutation with drug-resistant seizures it is the distinction that matters when ketogenic therapy comes up. Via PubMed. (PubMed, doi.org)
