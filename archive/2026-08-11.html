🌍 World
  • Ukraine's deep strikes turn deadly — A Ukrainian drone attack on the petrochemical hub of Nizhnekamsk in Russia's Tatarstan region — roughly 1,200 km from the border — killed 13 people and injured 78, among the deadliest long-range strikes of the war and a sign of how far Kyiv's reach now extends into Russia's energy infrastructure. Russia continued its own bombardment, with a glide-bomb strike on Zaporizhzhia wounding two dozen. (PBS NewsHour, U.S. News)
  • Netanyahu breaks with Trump on Gaza — Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu publicly rejected the 15-point Gaza framework from Trump's "Board of Peace," which paired a phased Israeli withdrawal with Hamas disarmament. Netanyahu said the IDF will not withdraw until Hamas fully disarms first — a rare open split with the US president just over a week after Trump announced the deal as a breakthrough. (NPR, Al Jazeera)
  • Hormuz reopening hits Iran's terms — A 60-day interim arrangement between Oman and Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is taking shape, but Iran is now demanding additional US concessions before vessels transit freely — inbound traffic overseen by Iran, outbound by Oman, before shifting to a central route. Washington had pushed for a mid-week announcement; the strait has been effectively closed since the February war. (CNN, Axios)

A Center for American Progress analysis found the broader $1.7 billion Guard deployments did not meaningfully reduce violent crime; Trump dismissed it as a "Radical Left SCAM." Protesters marked the anniversary calling to "Free DC." (Washington Post, The Hill, Al Jazeera)
  • Inflation reading lands Wednesday — The July Consumer Price Index is due Wednesday morning and is expected to show a modest 0.2% monthly rise, with the annual headline rate easing toward 3.4% and core around 2.5%. Economists broadly expect the Fed to hold its benchmark rate at 3.50%–3.75% through year-end, though a hotter-than-expected print would revive talk of hikes. (CNBC, Kiplinger)
