🇺🇸 US & Politics

  • The House shelves the Hegseth articles — Monday's brief had leadership braced for defections; the vote went the other way. The Republican-led House voted 211–204 on Tuesday to refer Representative Thomas Massie's eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, which effectively kills them for this Congress. Massie was the only Republican to vote against setting the articles aside; seven Republicans and eleven Democrats did not vote at all, more than the margin. Hegseth called the effort “a farce.” Democrats take the House majority in January and could revive the articles then, with much better odds of passage. (NPR, NBC News, Stars and Stripes)
  • Jim Jordan will lead House Republicans — Friday's brief had Mike Johnson stepping aside and both Steve Scalise and Jim Jordan expected to run. House Republicans chose Jordan, the Ohio congressman who chairs the Judiciary Committee, over Majority Leader Scalise by 106–73 in a secret ballot on Tuesday. He becomes minority leader when the 120th Congress convenes in January, where Democrats currently hold a 254–166 edge with a handful of races still uncalled. Senate Republicans separately kept John Thune as their leader. (PBS NewsHour, Axios, Roll Call)
  • The US is about to lose measles-free status — A Pan American Health Organization expert commission met in Washington from Tuesday through Friday to decide whether the United States still qualifies as having eliminated measles, a status it has held since 2000. The test is whether a single chain of transmission has run unbroken for 12 months or more, and reporting suggests the answer is yes. PAHO's director makes the final call, expected to be made public early next week. Losing the designation carries no formal penalty — it is a measure of how well vaccination coverage and outbreak response are working. Canada lost its status a year ago; Mexico and Bolivia are on PAHO's watch list. (CIDRAP, Scientific American, RTÉ)

⚡ Tech & AI

  • WORKFLOWPremiere 27.0 ships an AI assistant and generative audio — Adobe released Premiere 27.0 on Tuesday at MAX, rolling out to Creative Cloud subscribers now. Three pieces matter for an editor: the AI Assistant panel comes out of beta and will read your project and carry out plain-language requests; Generative Media now creates video, music, sound effects and ambience directly in the timeline, with Firefly plus third-party models including Veo, Kling, Runway and Luma; and Color Mode, a full grading workspace beside the timeline, is out of beta too.   • WORKFLOWApple starts charging for AI overage — The Creator Studio assistants covered here on November 4 now have a price attached when you use them heavily. Apple began selling one-time $49.99 “Intelligence Usage Packs” on Monday for subscribers who hit their monthly cap, bought inside the app under Intelligence Features → Show Usage → Buy More Usage. Logic Pro is the only app selling them so far, with Final Cut Pro and Pixelmator Pro expected to follow this month. Unused capacity rolls over monthly but expires a year after purchase and cannot be shared through Family Sharing. The honest gap: Apple does not publish how much work a pack actually buys — it varies by request complexity and server load — so there is no way to price a job against it in advance. Creator Studio itself remains $12.99 a month. (AppleInsider, Notebookcheck)
  • Apple is closing its support forums — Apple announced Tuesday that the Apple Support Community discussion boards shut permanently on Friday, November 20, ending a two-decade archive of user-written troubleshooting. Worth knowing if you have ever solved a Mac or Final Cut problem by landing on one of those threads from a search: that corpus is going away, and Apple is pointing people to its own support channels instead. Nothing to do about it, but it is a reason to save the page next time a fix you found there actually works. (MacRumors)

🌊 San Diego

  • Midway Rising clears the council unanimously — After a meeting that ran more than seven hours, the City Council voted 9–0 on Monday to approve the real estate agreements, zoning and permitting for Midway Rising, the roughly $4.2 billion redevelopment of the 48.5-acre Sports Arena site. The plan is a new 16,000-seat arena, park space, shops and restaurants and about 4,200 apartments, a large share of them income-restricted; Councilmember Kent Lee added an amendment meant to ensure the affordable units are net new rather than replacements. The council also approved an Enhanced Infrastructure Financing District, which the city must now form with the County over roughly the next year. Opponents are still warning about the terms the city agreed to. (NBC 7 San Diego, Fox 5 San Diego, ABC 10News)
  • A 400-megawatt data center draws a federal bill — San Diego's four Democratic House members say they will introduce legislation to stop a proposed 400-megawatt commercial data center on federal land at the NAVWAR site — which would be the largest in California — unless local government consents. The mechanism matters: the city has already passed a 45-day emergency pause on data centers, but it does not reach federal land, which is why the delegation went to Congress. The bill was held until the House returned this month and the text is not public yet, so accounts differ on whether it blocks the project outright or simply requires local sign-off. Manchester Financial Group, which proposed it, says it is one of several options and no agreements are in place; Representative Scott Peters wants the campus used for housing. (Times of San Diego, Voice of San Diego, CBS 8)
