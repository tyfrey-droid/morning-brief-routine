  • Eight die in a Brooklyn building collapse — A three-storey building at Flatbush and Bedford avenues in Flatbush collapsed into an adjacent construction excavation on Wednesday, killing eight people — two of them children, all eight since named by police — and injuring about 18, four of them critically.   • Mississippi's open Senate seat goes to Scott Colom — Monday's brief had the seat still uncalled. Democrat Scott Colom, a district attorney, has unseated Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith, making him Mississippi's first Black senator since Reconstruction and — because Hiram Revels and Blanche Bruce were chosen by the state legislature in the 1870s — the first elected to the seat by voters. It is the first Democratic win in the seat since 1982, in a state Trump carried by 23 points in 2024. Outlets describe the result as a projection; he has not been sworn in. (Roll Call, CNN)
  • USDA moves to narrow food-stamp eligibility — A proposed rule published Tuesday would limit automatic SNAP eligibility to households receiving direct cash assistance through TANF, ending the broad-based categorical eligibility states have used to extend the programme past its own income and asset limits. USDA's own estimate is that 3.2 million households containing 6.3 million people would no longer qualify once the rule is fully in effect in fiscal 2029, that 1.8 million children would lose automatic eligibility for free school meals, and that federal SNAP spending would fall by about $44.65bn over five years. A second rule issued the same day would tighten work requirements. Comments close December 10, and nothing changes unless and until a final rule is published. (Federal Register, NACo, Civil Eats)
