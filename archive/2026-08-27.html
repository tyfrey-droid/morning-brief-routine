  • A glacier collapse floods the Nepal–Tibet border — A glacier-and-rock collapse early Wednesday sent a wall of water and debris down the valleys straddling the Nepal–Tibet border, destroying villages, roads, bridges and hydropower plants. 🧬 Mito Research
  • A urine marker that tracks mutation load — Researchers at North-West University in South Africa, working with Radboud’s mitochondrial medicine center, Osaka and Tsukuba, ran untargeted urinary metabolomics on a mouse model carrying a pathogenic mtDNA deletion and found metabolites that change with mutation load — several rising sharply past roughly 60% heteroplasmy, the point where lactic acidemia and OXPHOS defects appear in that model. They then checked those metabolites in urine from patients carrying m.3243A>G and found the same direction of change across species. 2-hydroxyisovalerate (2-HIVA) was the strongest discriminator in both. What is new: heteroplasmy has been measurable only by tissue sampling, and tissue burden correlates poorly with how a child actually is. A validated urinary marker would mean tracking disease activity from a cup rather than a biopsy. It is a single cohort and needs replication, but it is a real step toward a non-invasive endpoint — the thing trials of MELAS drugs currently lack. (PubMed · DOI)
  • Sonlicromanol advances to a registrational trial — A new review of m.3243A>G MELAS therapeutics names where each candidate now stands, and two entries were not in any prior brief. Sonlicromanol (KH176) posted company-reported Phase IIb signals on cognition, mood and fatigue and has moved to KHENERFIN (NCT06451757), a Phase III registrational trial — the stage at which a result can support approval rather than just justify more study. KL1333, an NAD+ modulator, is in Phase II in the FALCON trial. Zagociguat and TTI-0102, both covered here in June, continue. The review also credits high-dose taurine (9–12 g/day) as the first regulatory-approved disease-modifying therapy for MELAS, on Phase III data showing complete prevention of stroke-like episodes in 60% of participants. Honest framing: this is a narrative review, not new data, and every drug listed targets downstream damage rather than the m.3243A>G mutation itself — the gene-editing work (mitoTALENs, DdCBE base editors, mitoARCUS) remains in patient-derived cells and animals. (PubMed · DOI)
