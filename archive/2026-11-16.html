🌍 World

  • Iran claims a mine campaign in Hormuz — Iran's navy and the Revolutionary Guard said over the weekend that a series of oil tankers struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, caught fire and in some cases sank — including a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude on Sunday. The IRGC put the weekend total at six vessels; Iran's state news agency IRNA counted five over 48 hours, and no statement has named a single ship or flag. None of the sinkings has been independently confirmed, and the IRGC frames the mining as retaliation for what it calls an illegal US blockade, warning shippers that a southern transit route through the strait has been mined. (The Jerusalem Post, OilPrice.com, Marine Insight)
  • Four allies press Israel on winter aid — The foreign ministers of Australia, Canada, Italy and the UK issued a joint statement Sunday urging Israel to admit roughly 150,000 tents and other winter supplies now held at the Gaza crossings, calling the situation “entirely reversible” with the first winter storms already causing flooding. Israel's crossings authority says 93,000 tents and 8,600 trucks of winter supplies have entered, that it is coordinating a winter plan with the UN and the US-led Board of Peace, and that reconstruction waits on Hamas disarming. Aid agencies put the number of tents actually in their hands far lower. (Jewish News, Global News)

🇺🇸 US & Politics

  • An overloaded router grounds the country — A telecommunications failure at the FAA's air route traffic control centres triggered a nationwide ground stop at 3:54 p.m. ET Friday, the third in the agency's history; it lifted at 8:15 p.m. ET, and the FAA says operators cancelled about 12,462 flights, though not all of those trace to the stop. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said control-centre routers were overwhelmed with data and began returning inconsistent readings, work tied to contractor L3Harris's conversion of the FAA's copper network to fibre; the White House said there was no sign of a “nefarious act.” Some controllers have publicly disputed that explanation, and operations returned to normal over the weekend. (FAA, CBS News, Al Jazeera)
  • A stuck rudder capsized the paddlewheeler — The NTSB said Monday that the 525-foot asphalt tanker Atlantic Asphalt suffered a steering failure — its rudder jammed in one position, pushing the ship toward the east bank — two minutes before it struck the docked cruise boat American Heritage in New Orleans on Friday. All 190 people aboard survived; the paddlewheeler took about 22 minutes to capsize and roughly 100 people were pulled from the Mississippi. Investigators recovered the voyage data recorder Saturday and say they do not yet know why the steering failed. The Port of New Orleans has sued the tanker's owners, putting its own damage above $1 million. (gCaptain, CNN, CBS News)
  • The asylum suspension reaches the court — The Supreme Court agreed Friday to decide whether the president may suspend asylum access at the southern border, taking up the administration's appeal of lower-court rulings that found the executive order — which describes the border situation as an “invasion” — unlawful. Argument is expected in spring 2027 with a decision by the end of the term in June. The court struck down the birthright-citizenship order in June but has separately allowed the administration to resume turning asylum seekers away at the border before they enter. (SCOTUSblog, PBS NewsHour, NBC News)

⚡ Tech & AI

  • WORKFLOW Claude sits in on the call — Anthropic launched Claude Meetings, which joins a Google Meet call and turns the discussion into notes, plans and tasks while people are still talking, rather than producing a transcript afterwards. For a one-person shop the use is client calls and creative reviews: the brief, the revision list and the follow-ups come out of the call itself instead of an evening spent writing them up. It is in beta on Team and Enterprise plans, so it is not on an individual Pro subscription yet, and anything said on a client call goes to a cloud service — worth a line in your own agreement before you switch it on. (TechCrunch)
  • Microsoft's mail stopped for a day — Outlook, Teams and Exchange Online went down worldwide from November 12 into November 14, with Downdetector logging some 7.4 million reports; Microsoft says the fault was introduced during a network capacity upgrade and surfaced after a later network change. Most UK MPs lost inbox access, House staff told Politico a day of legislative work was simply gone, and Maersk warned shipping customers it could not send notifications. Accounts of the length vary from about 12 hours to 38 depending on region and service, and the practical lesson is the familiar one: a cloud mailbox is a single point of failure. (GeekWire, NBC News, CNN)
  • Vulcan loses forty satellites — United Launch Alliance's Vulcan rocket failed about ten minutes after lifting off from Cape Canaveral on Sunday, destroying all 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites aboard — the first outright mission failure in ULA's history and the largest batch the constellation had flown at once. Spaceflight Now reports the anomaly occurred during the planned burn of the Centaur upper stage; Northrop Grumman said its solid boosters performed normally and separated before it. The flight was the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon has bought, and it leaves the service roughly 390 satellites in orbit against an FCC milestone of 1,600. (Spaceflight Now, Space.com, Engadget)
  • Apple's first smart display goes on sale — HomeView, a 7-inch counter-or-wall display that uses facial recognition to show a personalised home screen for up to six people in a household, went on sale Friday alongside the fourth-generation Apple TV 4K, both at $299 and both open for pre-order since October 13. The new Apple TV runs the A19, which Apple rates at up to 50% faster CPU and twice the GPU of the outgoing model, adds a dedicated AV1 decoder and a Siri Remote with Find My, and supports four-speaker surround with two HomePod mini 2s. One caveat if you set HomeView up this week: a Siri fault stops it controlling smart-home hardware such as locks, and the fix is not due until next month. (MacRumors, MacRumors)

🌊 San Diego

  • The council moves to drop Flock — San Diego's city council is expected to vote today to end its use of Flock Safety's automated licence-plate reader network, and Mayor Todd Gloria said Friday he would not veto that result. The reversal follows reporting that police ran plate searches involving a domestic violence victim, which drew criticism at the council's last hearing on the contract. (Fox 5 San Diego)
  • Record ocean heat off the coast — Sea surface temperatures off San Diego are running at record levels for mid-November, and researchers say the warmer water can load winter storms with additional moisture and heavier rainfall. The same heat is putting thermal stress on local kelp forests. It is the likeliest explanation for buoy water readings in the mid-70s this late in the year. (Times of San Diego)
  • Pacific Beach pier closed by surf — Large weekend surf closed piers up and down the California coast, including Pacific Beach's, and pushed water over the beach and up a staircase at La Jolla Cove. Waves above 50 feet were recorded in San Mateo County. Conditions eased Sunday afternoon, but a high surf advisory for the San Diego and Orange county coasts runs until 4 a.m. Wednesday and forecasters expect large surf all week. (ABC News, Las Vegas Review-Journal)
