🇺🇸 US & Politics

  • The Thanksgiving storm cuts power to hundreds of thousands — The coastal storm forecasters had been tracking all week arrived on the holiday itself, leaving more than 800,000 customers without power across the Midwest and Northeast by Thursday night, with the running total approaching a million; Pennsylvania and New Jersey were worst hit and several New Jersey bridges closed. Central Park recorded 1.82 inches of rain, its wettest Thanksgiving on record, and a tide gauge at Duck, North Carolina set a preliminary all-time high, topping the mark from Hurricane Isabel in 2003. Saturday’s forecast is mostly sunny and near 55° with gusts in the 20s, comfortably inside the limit. (CBS News, Axios, NPR)

(Adobe, NPR)

  • Nvidia pauses AI server shipments to four countries — Nvidia has told its server-building partners to stop shipping AI systems to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, according to Financial Times reporting picked up this week. The pause follows new US export rules that took effect last week requiring licences for advanced chips bound for several Southeast Asian countries, aimed at closing a route by which Chinese firms rent access through regional data centres.   • King tides set a La Jolla record — Wednesday morning’s king tide peaked at 8.11 feet at La Jolla, breaking the 7.98-foot record set in late October, but the low-lying coastal communities that had been sandbagged and watched all week came through without damaging flooding.   • A judge limits ICE re-detentions at check-ins — District Judge Dana Sabraw ruled Tuesday that ICE must make an individualised finding that a person’s circumstances have changed enough to make them a flight risk or a danger before re-detaining them when they appear for a scheduled check-in. The order covers San Diego and Imperial counties and is preliminary; if the court ultimately finds the practice violates due process or the Administrative Procedure Act, a permanent injunction could follow for the certified class.   • Gun violence keeps falling countywide — The county’s annual gun violence report, out this week, records a 28% drop in firearm homicides and a 43.7% drop in firearm trauma cases — 213 in 2022 down to 120 in 2025 — alongside roughly a 60% fall in ghost-gun recoveries. Firearm suicides are the exception, up 6.6% year over year, and the city of San Diego still accounts for nearly half of all firearm-related emergency department and trauma-centre admissions, followed by El Cajon, Chula Vista and Escondido. (County News Center, NBC San Diego)
