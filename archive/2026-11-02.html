🧬 Mito Research

  • Why mutant mtDNA takes over a muscle fibre — A team led from UCL's Queen Square Institute of Neurology, publishing in Cell Reports on October 29 with collaborators at Newcastle, Stockholm and Oxford, followed mouse muscle over time and found that depleted mitochondrial DNA first compensates by making more transcripts per genome, which preserves the respiratory chain — and then, months later, transcripts fall away, deletions appear, and respiratory-deficient fibres emerge. The counterintuitive part is that the fibres carrying deletions end up with far more mtDNA than normal, and every one of them shows raised Hsp60, a pattern the group also saw in human mitochondrial myopathy. They argue that depletion inadvertently creates an Hsp60-high cellular state that lets a mutant genome expand clonally. What matters for a MELAS family is the mechanism rather than the model: clonal expansion is how a mutant load becomes high enough in a tissue to cause symptoms, so a molecular state that permits it is a candidate thing to interfere with. The caveat is real — this is deletion mutations in a mouse depletion model, not the m.3243A>G point mutation, and nothing here is a treatment. (Cell Reports — DOI, indexed on PubMed)
