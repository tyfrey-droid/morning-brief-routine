⚡ Tech & AI
  • AI starts advising air traffic control today — The FAA begins a limited rollout today of SMART — Strategic Management of Airspace, Route, and Trajectories — in the crowded airspace over Washington, D.C., the first step toward national deployment. The system cross-references airline schedules, runway capacity and weather to flag congestion and conflicts before they happen, stretching the planning window from about 15 minutes to two hours; it advises controllers rather than replacing their decisions. Air Space Intelligence built it under a contract worth $875 million over 12 years. The caveat the coverage keeps returning to: the agency is still thousands of controllers short, and no software fixes that. (TechCrunch, NPR, NewsNation)
  • Apple’s September hardware is all on shelves now — The iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4, and AirPods 5 all went on sale Friday, which closes out everything announced at the Sept. 9 event except the foldable iPhone Duo — that one pre-orders Oct. 16 and ships Oct. 23. If you are staying on current hardware, the thing to know about iOS 27 is that its headline feature, the rebuilt conversational Siri, requires an iPhone 15 Pro or newer; the speed work, the Liquid Glass transparency slider and the rest of the release apply more broadly. (MacRumors, 9to5Mac)

🔋 Electric Vehicles
  • The EV price gap narrows to 9.4% — The average new electric vehicle sold for $54,813 in August, down 2.7% from a year earlier, while the average new vehicle of any kind rose almost 2% to $50,089 — putting EVs 9.4% above the market, against a 16% gap at this time last year. The more interesting half is that it is happening with less help: average EV incentives fell from about $8,200 to $6,600 over the same year. Cox Automotive’s Stephanie Valdez-Streaty: “The parity story isn’t just about EV prices coming down, it’s also about EVs becoming less dependent on incentives.” (InsideEVs)
  • An AM radio mandate heads to the Senate — (general EV news) The House passed the AM Radio for Every Vehicle Act by voice vote, which would require an AM tuner at no extra cost in every new vehicle sold in the US, electric ones included, with the rule generally taking effect within two years of a DOT report to Congress. EVs are the reason the bill exists: the rapidly switching currents in an EV’s power electronics bleed interference straight into the AM band, and several automakers dropped the tuner rather than shield around it. The Senate has not acted. (InsideEVs, Rep. Pallone’s office)
