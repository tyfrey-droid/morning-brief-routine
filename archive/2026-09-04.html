🌍 World
  • Berlin formally blames Moscow for a drone attack — After a month-long investigation, the German government said the explosive-laden drone that landed near a Ukrainian cargo aircraft at Leipzig/Halle airport in early August was flown by agents working on behalf of Russian state entities. The drone failed to detonate and was defused by a police bomb squad; two more were found at or near the airport in the following weeks. Leipzig/Halle is a hub for both Ukraine resupply and NATO airlift. Berlin responded by closing the Russian House in Berlin and the Russian consulate in Bonn. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte backed the measures; President Putin called them a “grave mistake” and accused Germany of falsifying evidence. (NPR, CNN, Defense News, Al Jazeera)
  • Germany votes Sunday on a far-right first — Saxony-Anhalt holds its state election on Sept. 6, and polls have the AfD comfortably ahead — in some surveys near an outright majority. If its candidate Ulrich Siegmund, a 35-year-old former marketer whose campaign has leaned heavily on TikTok, forms a government, he would be the first far-right state premier in Germany since 1945. An absolute majority would also give the AfD its first seats in the Bundesrat, the chamber representing the states, with further elections in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Berlin later this year. (NPR, KPBS, CSIS)
  • Congo’s Ebola deaths pass 3,000 — The Democratic Republic of the Congo reported more than 6,200 confirmed cases and over 3,000 deaths as of Sept. 2, less than four months after the World Health Organization declared the outbreak a public health emergency of international concern on May 16. It is the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record and, the WHO says, on track to become the deadliest. The virus involved is the rarer Bundibugyo strain, for which the licensed Zaire-strain vaccines and monoclonal treatments are not established. (The Washington Post, NBC News, The Hill)
  • Milei reopens the Falklands fight — Argentina’s president said he will send Congress an urgent bill widening sanctions on companies involved in oil exploration around the Falkland Islands, extending penalties beyond operators to their suppliers, shareholders and directors. His target is the Sea Lion project about 140 km north of the islands — run by Israel’s Navitas Petroleum and Britain’s Rockhopper Exploration, with an estimated 1.7 billion barrels — which he called a “concrete and urgent danger” advancing without Argentine permission. The timing follows President Trump suggesting Washington could revisit its long-standing ambiguity on sovereignty while accepting de facto British rule. Britain has controlled the islands since 1833. (CNN, CNBC)

🎥 Filmmaking Gear
  • DJI puts a 1-inch square sensor in a 360 camera — DJI launched the Osmo 360 II on Thursday ahead of its show debut at IFA Berlin, Sept. 4–8. The notable part is the sensor: a 1-inch square design that keeps the same 360° field as a conventional 1-inch rectangular chip while claiming 14.5 stops of dynamic range, roughly double the brightness latitude of the previous generation, plus 8K/60fps capture, replaceable lenses and a reworked low-light noise-reduction pass. 🧬 Mito Research
  • A strip test for m.3243A>G mutation load — Researchers report a CRISPR-based assay, VISTA, that reads m.3243A>G — the MELAS variant — using an FnCas12a enzyme tuned to distinguish the mutant from the normal sequence, with results legible either by fluorescence or on a lateral-flow strip like a home test. Across 74 de-identified blood samples the readouts classified mutant-carrying samples with an area under the curve above 0.9. The honest limits: the strip gives a relative ranking rather than an absolute heteroplasmy percentage, it still needs a DNA amplification step first, it was validated on blood rather than the muscle or urine where load runs higher, and it is a laboratory method, not an approved clinical test. What it points toward is heteroplasmy monitoring that does not require sending samples out for sequencing — which matters if any of the therapies now in trials ever needs load tracked over time. Via PubMed. (PubMed, DOI)
