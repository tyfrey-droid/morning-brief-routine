🌍 World
  • The AfD wins a German state outright — Alternative für Deutschland took 43.8% in Sunday’s Saxony-Anhalt election, more than double the 17.2% for the incumbent Christian Democrats and the party’s strongest state result yet. It still fell short of governing alone: 39 of 83 seats, three below the 42 needed for a majority. Every other party maintains the “firewall” against coalition with the AfD, which has never governed in any of Germany’s 16 states or federally, so who forms the next state government is unresolved. (NPR, CNN, Al Jazeera)
  • US envoys carry a plan from Moscow to Kyiv — Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner spent more than three hours with Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin on Saturday, then flew to Kyiv on Sunday to meet Volodymyr Zelenskyy — their first visit to the Ukrainian capital since the 2022 invasion. Both sides called the meetings substantive, and Witkoff said he was “very encouraged.” Putin’s foreign-policy adviser Yuri Ushakov said the Moscow talks ranged beyond the battlefield into possible US–Russia joint economic ventures, but named nothing agreed, and Putin gave no public sign of compromise. Washington wants the war ended before winter; Kyiv’s side has said Moscow is unlikely to negotiate seriously before 2027. (NPR, Al Jazeera, NBC News)
  • The Iran conflict becomes a tanker war — US forces destroyed one Iranian oil tanker and permanently disabled two others near Kharg Island over the weekend — the first American strikes on Iranian tankers — after the Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles toward a US aircraft carrier and a destroyer. Neither ship was hit and no crew were hurt. Iran said it then struck three tankers it called unauthorised transits of the Strait of Hormuz plus three other US-linked vessels, and Tehran is moving to declare a restricted zone near the strait. Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Sunday the US may not reach a deal constraining Iran’s nuclear programme, with the conflict now in its seventh month. (NPR, CNN, Al Jazeera)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • Five killed as an Amazon cargo jet overruns a Miami runway — Prime Air Flight 7598, a 32-year-old Boeing 767 flown for Amazon by North Carolina–based 21 Air, ran off the runway at Miami International just before 2 p.m. Sunday while landing from San Juan. It came to rest in a field past the airport fence, striking several vehicles on Northwest 42nd Avenue and sending up heavy black smoke. Five people were killed and five injured; more than 60 Miami-Dade Fire Rescue units responded. The FAA and NTSB are investigating. (NPR, The Washington Post, CBS News)
  • A third run at the Supreme Court over mail ballots — The administration filed an emergency application Sunday asking the justices to let the Postal Service enforce a new mail-ballot rule while the case proceeds, after US District Judge Indira Talwani in Boston extended a block on it last week. The rule would require states to hand USPS lists of mail-ballot recipients and put unique barcodes on outbound and return envelopes, and would let the Postal Service refuse ballots that fail those standards or belong to voters not on the lists. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson, who handles emergency appeals from Massachusetts, told challengers to respond by 4 p.m. Wednesday. Some states have already started mailing ballots for the Nov. 3 midterms. (NBC News, SCOTUSblog)

Microsoft says it leads the FLEURS benchmark across 60 languages with a 5.2% average word error rate and runs five to ten times faster than transcription models from Google, OpenAI and ElevenLabs. It adds speaker diarization, word-level timestamps, keyword biasing, automatic language identification and mid-sentence language switching, and is available through Microsoft Foundry and OpenRouter. 🌊 San Diego
  • The mosque shooter’s parents sue his treatment provider — The parents of one of the two gunmen in the May 18 Islamic Center of San Diego attack, which killed three people, have sued Park Mental Health for negligence and wrongful death. Their 18-year-old son had lived at the provider’s Normal Heights residential facility since late April with diagnoses including autism, social anxiety and severe depression with psychotic episodes. Times of San Diego reported that the house manager has video of him leaving the property around 8:24 p.m. the night before the shooting; the complaint says that four days earlier the FBI called his mother to say he was on the dark web discussing suicide and school shootings. (KPBS, Times of San Diego)
  • A widow sues Poway Unified over a school-bus death — Cindy Steiner is suing the district over the death of her husband Rene, struck by a Poway Unified bus while walking his dogs in Rancho Peñasquitos on March 10; he died 18 days later. The complaint alleges a long-standing district practice of routing buses onto residential streets to avoid congested arterials, and states that since 2019 the district has documented bus-related incidents at 118 separate locations in Rancho Peñasquitos and Sabre Springs. The driver faces a misdemeanor vehicular-manslaughter charge and was cited for failing to yield to a pedestrian in a crosswalk; his next hearing is Oct. 27. (Times of San Diego, FOX 5 San Diego)
  • A 50th nonstop from San Diego, to Baja — Alaska Airlines will fly nonstop from San Diego International to Loreto, in Baja California Sur on the Sea of Cortez, starting Dec. 19 — twice weekly on Wednesdays and Saturdays through April 28, 2027, on Embraer E175s operated by SkyWest. It is the airline’s 50th nonstop destination from San Diego, announced alongside its 40th year serving the city, and it will be the only nonstop on the route. (Times of San Diego, Alaska Airlines)
🌺 Kauai
  • A hurricane warning is up for Kauai — Lowell, a major hurricane, is tracking north just west of the island, and Kauai and Niihau are now under a hurricane warning after a tropical storm warning was issued Saturday. Forecasters expect an additional 6 to 10 inches of rain on Kauai with local totals to 14 inches, sustained winds from 35 to more than 55 mph with higher gusts, and storm surge raising water up to 3 feet above normally dry ground near the coast; hurricane conditions were expected to begin this afternoon or evening. County parks closed ahead of the storm, and Labor Day events including the Lihue church breakfast and the Filipino Chamber of Commerce scholarship golf tournament at Wailua were cancelled. (Honolulu Star-Advertiser, Maui Now, The Garden Island)

  • US drone tariffs took effect Sept. 3 — A White House proclamation imposed duties of 25% to 100% on imported drones and components. Camera drones at or under 25 kg without thermal imaging draw 25%; anything heavier, anything carrying a thermal camera, plus docking stations and a list of critical parts, draw 100%. Drones from Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Switzerland, Liechtenstein and the EU are capped at 15% and UK products at 10%. Airframe parts — propellers, rotors, undercarriages — follow at 25% on Feb. 9, 2027. Paying the duty does not restore DJI’s access to US retail; the existing restrictions stand. (CineD, Imaging Resource)
  • GoPro merges into an optics firm and pivots to defense — GoPro and privately held optical-photonics company Starman Optical signed a definitive merger agreement on Sept. 1. The $285 million deal recapitalises GoPro and clears its debt, repositioning the 24-year-old camera maker toward AI infrastructure, government and defense markets while it continues running the consumer camera and subscription business. (Newsshooter)

Tesla notified the agency it had self-certified the vehicles as compliant with all applicable federal motor vehicle safety standards; NHTSA says it will examine the process and technical data behind that determination, including Tesla’s conclusion that certain standards do not apply to the vehicle at all.
