  • A control-room decision caused Spain's rail disaster — A Renfe AVE travelling the Madrid–Seville line struck a stopped Iryo train near Toledo on Wednesday morning, killing at least 68 people and sending about 58 more to hospital. Transport minister Óscar Puente has attributed the crash to what he called an “inexplicable” decision by the central rail control centre to let a train resume moving after a safety system malfunctioned, and said the drivers bear no responsibility. It is Spain's second fatal high-speed crash this year, after a January collision near Adamuz killed 46. (CNBC, NBC News)

Separately, the IRGC said four tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday; Washington has not confirmed that account, and President Trump has accused Iran of inventing ship attacks to lift oil prices. ⚡ Tech & AI

  • WORKFLOW MotionVFX comes built into Final Cut Pro — Apple's Creator Studio update, announced Monday and shipping as a free App Store update in November, folds MotionVFX effects, titles and templates directly into Final Cut Pro for Creator Studio subscribers — a library most editors have been paying MotionVFX separately for. It also opens the Content Hub's video assets inside Final Cut, adds Pixelmator Pro round-tripping so a still on the timeline can be opened, edited and saved straight back, and adds keyframe easing. The catch for anyone who bought Final Cut outright: the perpetual licence stays available at $300, but the bundled MotionVFX content follows the subscription, so a one-time buyer still needs their own MotionVFX plan. Requires macOS 26.6 or later. (Apple, PetaPixel, DPReview)
  • The Mac gets a touchscreen, at a price — Wednesday's event produced two separate MacBook Pro lines. The headline machine is the first touchscreen Mac: 14- and 16-inch models with a tandem-OLED “PROLED XDR” display rated to 2,000 nits peak and 1,000 sustained, a 10–120Hz refresh range, thinner bodies and a Dynamic Island in place of the notch. It runs M5 Pro or M5 Max — not the newer chip — and starts at $2,899 for the 14-inch and $3,399 for the 16-inch, rising to $4,499 and $4,799 with M5 Max. Quietly alongside it, the ordinary 14-inch MacBook Pro moved to M6 at $1,999, with a 12-core CPU and 12-core GPU, Wi-Fi 7, and Apple's claims of 30 per cent faster graphics and 25 per cent faster AI work than M5. Both ship November 6. (Apple, TechRepublic, MacRumors)
