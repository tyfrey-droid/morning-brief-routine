🧬 Mito Research
  • Mount Sinai makes the case for PDE5 inhibitors — An editorial in Molecular Genetics and Metabolism from Eva Morava, Tamas Kozicz and colleagues at the Icahn School of Medicine argues for phosphodiesterase-5 inhibition — sildenafil and tadalafil, both long-approved drugs — as an off-label therapy in mitochondrial disease. What sits behind it: a small off-label series of six patients with primary mitochondrial disease showed lasting improvement or stabilization over months (less dystonia, better motor skills, coordination and strength) with no reported adverse effects, and their fibroblasts showed improved respiratory function. Separately, a stem-cell-based screen published in Cell this year surfaced sildenafil as a lead on clinical-safety grounds, correcting membrane-potential defects in Leigh syndrome brain organoids. This is a repurposing case built on case series and cell work, not a controlled trial — but it concerns drugs a neurologist can already prescribe today, which is rare in this field. (PubMed, doi.org, Cell)
  • Migraine is far more common in MELAS than thought — A seven-center Chinese study of 48 genetically confirmed patients with mitochondrial disease, published in Headache, found a one-year migraine rate of 48% against 9% in the general population. Epilepsy raised the odds independently, as did higher resting blood lactate. Among the 29 MELAS patients, all 12 who had acute headache accompanying focal deficits or seizures met migraine criteria, half with typical aura, and they rated their pain higher than the others. This is observational, not a treatment finding, and 48 patients is small — but for families it reframes headache during a stroke-like episode as a distinct, recognizable and treatable symptom rather than background noise, which matters most for a child who cannot report pain. (PubMed, doi.org)
