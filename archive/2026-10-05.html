🧬 Mito Research
  • Anakinra calmed an inflammatory storm during a stroke-like episode — A three-centre case series in Molecular Genetics and Metabolism, from Baylor and Texas Children’s, the NIH’s National Human Genome Research Institute and Mount Sinai, describes a teenager with m.3243A>G MELAS who developed a sterile, interferon-driven hyperinflammatory syndrome during a stroke-like episode. He met only two of the eight formal criteria for hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, so the inflammation would have been missed by the usual threshold — but his soluble IL-2 receptor, IL-6, IL-10, CXCL9 and spinal-fluid neopterin were all markedly elevated. Intravenous immunoglobulin barely helped. Anakinra, an interleukin-1 blocker already licensed for other conditions, produced sustained improvement in both his symptoms and those markers, and he was alive at 15 when the paper was written. Two other patients in the series, with PDHA1 and POLG disease, met full HLH criteria and died. What is new here is a measurable inflammatory signature during a stroke-like episode and a drug a clinician can already prescribe that one patient clearly responded to; what it is not is a trial — three patients, reviewed retrospectively from their charts. (Molecular Genetics and Metabolism)
