🌍 World
  • Colombia quake toll passes 270 — Rescue crews entered what officials called the "final phase" of the search Thursday after Monday's magnitude-7.4 earthquake in western Colombia, the country's strongest in a century. The confirmed death toll has climbed past 270 with more than 3,800 injured, hundreds still missing and over 12,000 homes destroyed; Colombia's Geological Service has logged more than 70 aftershocks. (France 24, NPR)
  • Total eclipse crosses Iceland and Spain — Mainland Europe saw its first total solar eclipse since 1999 on Wednesday evening, with the path of totality running from eastern Greenland across western Iceland into northern Spain and a corner of Portugal. Totality lasted under two and a half minutes and reached Spain — its first in more than a century — with the sun low on the horizon near sunset. (PBS NewsHour, Astronomy)
  • Iran says Hormuz stays shut — Tehran publicly disputed US suggestions that a reopening is close, with the Persian Gulf Strait Authority saying the Strait of Hormuz "remains blocked and will not be reopened until Iran's conditions are accepted" — among them lifting the naval blockade and sanctions, and compensation for damages. Transit traffic has fallen to near three-month lows, a reversal from last week's reports of an imminent 60-day interim arrangement. (CNBC, Fortune)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • Judge tosses Harvard antisemitism suit — US District Judge Richard Stearns in Boston dismissed the Trump administration's March lawsuit alleging Harvard failed to protect Jewish and Israeli students, finding the government had not plausibly shown an ongoing violation of federal civil rights law. Stearns wrote that the cited incidents came largely from the 2023-24 school year and that later ones were "too isolated and episodic," and rejected the bid to cut off and claw back federal grants — Congress meant Title VI enforcement "to incentivize" compliance, he wrote, "not to penalize a wayward funding recipient." (NPR, CNN, The Harvard Crimson)
  • Inflation cools slightly to 3.4% — July CPI rose 0.1% on the month — a shade under the 0.2% economists expected — putting annual inflation at 3.4%, down from 3.5% in June. Core inflation rose 0.2% for an annual 2.5%. Shelter accounted for about two-thirds of the headline increase while energy fell 1.5% on the month, though it remains 14.7% above a year ago. Inflation continues to run ahead of wage growth, roughly 3.2%. (CNBC, NBC News, Bureau of Labor Statistics)
  • Kennedy Center adds Trump's name — The board Trump fired and restocked with allies voted 20-3 Thursday to rename the complex "The John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts Restored and Renovated by President Donald J. Trump," inscribe his name in the marble facade and rename the grounds "President Donald J. Trump Plaza." The same vote closes the main building for up to two years for a $250 million renovation, leaving only the newer Reach expansion open for limited events. A judge ruled months ago that separate Trump signage had to come off the marquee. (NPR, Washington Post, NBC News)

The standard iPhone 18, the 18e and the Air 2 are held back to roughly March 2027, splitting Apple's iPhone launches across two seasons for the first time. (MacRumors, 9to5Mac)
🏀 Sports
  • Portland council backs $120M for Moda — Two days after Multnomah County signed on, Portland's City Council voted 8-4 Wednesday to approve a non-binding term sheet committing up to $120 million toward the roughly $600 million Moda Center renovation, plus $275 million in maintenance on the city-owned arena over the next two decades. A late amendment asks the Blazers for $3.1 million in annual rent indexed to inflation, on top of $3 million a year in lieu of property taxes. It is a visible demotion after years near the top of the league's TV priorities, with a roster returning essentially intact from a 37-win season. Curry and company will still play a 14th straight Christmas Day game, and open NBA Cup group play Oct. 30 at home against Dončić's Lakers. (The San Francisco Standard, NBC Bay Area)

The closures had drawn steady complaints about people relieving themselves outside the locked facilities. (KPBS)
