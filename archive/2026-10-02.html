🌍 World
  • Passengers stop a cockpit attack mid-flight — Flydubai FZ1073, a Dubai–Tel Aviv 737 MAX carrying about 180 people, made an emergency landing at Tabuk, Saudi Arabia on Wednesday after the first officer stabbed the captain and the aircraft lost altitude sharply. Israeli passengers and crew forced the cockpit door and subdued him; off-duty flydubai pilots travelling as passengers helped land the plane, and no passengers were hurt. Roosevelt’s arrival would put three US carriers in the Middle East at once. (The Hill, The War Zone)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • Tennessee execution fails; inmate survives — Christa Pike, whose scheduled execution this brief noted Wednesday, was still alive after staff administered two doses of pentobarbital over about an hour; witnesses said she remained audibly breathing when they were escorted out, and she was taken to a hospital. Her attorneys said Thursday she is in critical condition and receiving life-saving care, with no known prognosis. The Death Penalty Information Center says no US prisoner in the modern era has previously survived an administration of lethal-injection drugs. Tennessee has since called off its remaining scheduled executions. (The Hill, NBC News, PBS NewsHour)
  • $500 ACA refund checks go out — The administration began mailing $500 checks Wednesday to more than 950,000 people who bought unsubsidized coverage on the federal marketplace in the 30 states that use it, each accompanied by a letter from Trump saying the prior administration had overcharged them to fund HealthCare.gov. The refunds return a portion of the exchange user fees built into premiums. Health-policy analysts dispute the “overcharge” framing, noting those fees pay for the website, its call center and the navigator groups that help people enroll. (The Washington Post, CBS News, The White House)
⚡ Tech & AI
  • Frontier model prices converge at $2/$10 — Two releases days apart landed on identical pricing. OpenAI shipped GPT-6.1 Sol at DevDay on September 29 at $2 per million input tokens and $10 output — one-fifth of GPT-6 Astra’s rate, with cached input at $0.10 — and says it nearly matches Astra on agentic coding and computer use. Google then unveiled Gemini 4 Argon on Wednesday at the same $2/$10, claiming records on software-engineering and professional-task benchmarks. The difference is availability: Sol went out to all paid ChatGPT tiers and the API immediately, while Argon is phased, starting with cybersecurity partners and government pre-release safety evaluations, with no broad launch date announced. (OpenAI, The Next Web, Engadget)
  • Apple’s October lineup takes shape — Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman reports Apple plans new Home products around October 13, with a redesigned MacBook Pro — its first with a touchscreen — following shortly after, possibly slipping to November. Apple has separately confirmed iOS 27.1 will ship with the new iPhone this month, and iOS 27.2, already in testing, is expected before the end of October; it extends Siri’s AI features to French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish. Treat the hardware timing as reporting rather than announcement. (9to5Mac, 9to5Mac)
  • A geothermal plant built in 23 months — Fervo Energy began selling power from the first 33 MW block of its Cape Station project in Utah on September 30, the first greenfield enhanced-geothermal development to reach contractual commercial operation. Enhanced geothermal drills deeper than conventional plants to reach hotter rock, which widens where it can be built; the 23-month build is the number that matters, and Fervo is targeting 18 months for later blocks. Two more blocks are being commissioned toward about 100 MW by January 2027, with Google and Southern California Edison among the contracted buyers. (TechCrunch, Fervo Energy)
