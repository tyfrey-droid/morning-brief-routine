🧬 Mito Research
  • Real-world drug safety data in mito disease — Neurologists at Turku University Hospital reviewed roughly 1,000 medication entries across 12 years of hospital stays and prescriptions for 44 genetically confirmed mitochondrial disease patients — the first systematic look at what this population is actually being given. Two findings are reassuring: no drugs generally considered contraindicated in mitochondrial disease were prescribed, and no high-risk drug–drug interactions turned up. The useful caution is a named list of commonly used drugs with potential off-target mitochondrial effects — metformin, beta-blockers, statins, ciprofloxacin, fluoxetine, ibuprofen and certain anti-seizure medications — in a population whose impaired energy metabolism may make it more vulnerable to them. The authors also flag heavy analgesic use, arguing pain in mitochondrial disease is under-recognized. Mostly adult cohort (mean age 50, range 12–83), so it does not map directly onto a child, but it is a concrete conversation to have with a treating neurologist. (Neurol Sci, via PubMed · DOI)
