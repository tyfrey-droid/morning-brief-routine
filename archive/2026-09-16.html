🌍 World
  • Kosovo’s first president is convicted — The Kosovo Specialist Chambers in The Hague found Hashim Thaci guilty on four counts of war crimes Wednesday and sentenced him to 25 years, holding him responsible as Kosovo Liberation Army commander for the murder of 96 political opponents and suspected collaborators during the 1998–99 war with Serbia, alongside torture and illegal detention. Thaci went on to become prime minister and then president, resigning in 2020 to face the indictment. Thousands watched the verdict read live in Pristina, where many Kosovo Albanians still regard the KLA as liberators. (Al Jazeera, NPR, Euronews)
  • NATO downs a drone over Lithuania — Italian jets flying NATO’s Baltic Air Policing mission shot down a drone near Pratkunai, about 90km north of Vilnius, early Tuesday — the first such intercept over Lithuania. Officials said it entered from Belarus and likely carried explosives. The same day Denmark summoned Russia’s ambassador after a Russian warship fired two emergency flares at a Danish helicopter in the Baltic; Moscow’s envoy countered that Danish helicopters had manoeuvred dangerously near Russian vessels. Poland also recovered a drone from its waters. (France 24, Anchorage Daily News, U.S. News)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • The mail-ballot rule is dead for November — The Supreme Court refused to let the Postal Service enforce its new mail-voting regulation while litigation continues, ending the administration’s third attempt in two weeks to revive it. The rule would have required states to file lists of mail-ballot recipients through a federal portal that does not yet exist, imposed barcode standards on envelopes, and let USPS refuse ballots failing either test. Clarence Thomas and Samuel Alito noted dissents. Trump called the order “a big loss for Republicans” and criticised his own three appointees. (NBC News, CBS News, CNBC)
  • The Air Force says it has weapons in orbit — Air Force Secretary Troy Meink told the Air, Space and Cyber Conference on Monday that the United States “now has on-orbit space control weapons capable of defending the Joint Force against hostile adversary action” — the first direct official acknowledgment after decades in which the capability was assumed but never confirmed. Pressed afterward, Meink said the wording was deliberate and declined to say what the weapons are, how many are up there, or when they launched. (Military Times, SpaceNews, CNN)
⚡ Tech & AI
  • Three rival labs are talking safety — OpenAI policy chief Chris Lehane said Tuesday that the company has been coordinating with Anthropic and Google DeepMind on AI safety for several weeks, and that he sees no need for an antitrust waiver to do it. It is the concrete follow-through on the slow-down argument Dario Amodei made publicly over the weekend, and the first time the three leading labs have confirmed sustained joint work rather than parallel policy statements. What the coordination actually commits any of them to has not been spelled out. (TechCrunch, Bloomberg)
  • OpenAI’s IPO slides to 2027 — Sam Altman told Fortune the company will not list this year — “Given everything happening with safety, right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public” — pushing the window into 2027. OpenAI filed confidentially in June without setting a date. Altman pointed to unfinished safety and alignment work rather than market conditions, which is a notable thing for a company reportedly seeking one of the largest valuations in listing history to say out loud. (Fortune, TechCrunch)

🌊 San Diego
  • Poway bans AI data centres outright — The Poway City Council voted Tuesday to prohibit third-party companies from building standalone data centres for artificial-intelligence workloads anywhere in the city, following a July directive from Mayor Steve Vaus and Councilwoman Anita Edmondson. Staff cited water and electricity draw heavy enough to strain local infrastructure and push utility rates, plus air-quality, noise and fire-risk concerns. It is one of the first outright municipal bans in the county rather than a moratorium or a zoning restriction. (Times of San Diego, KPBS)
  • The city adopts a definition of Islamophobia — San Diego’s City Council voted unanimously Tuesday to adopt a working definition of Islamophobia, months after two attackers killed three men at a city mosque. The measure is declaratory rather than regulatory — it commits the city to a shared vocabulary for identifying anti-Muslim discrimination rather than creating a new penalty — and supporters framed it as the council taking a formal moral position. (KPBS)
  • 101 Ash gets another rewrite — The council amended its deal to convert the long-troubled 101 Ash Street office tower into affordable housing, extending the ground lease from 60 to 65 years, raising the unit count from 250 to 252, and moving the developer’s interest rate to the city from 4% simple to 5% compound. The building has been empty and litigated over since asbestos forced workers out in 2020. (KPBS)
🌺 Kauai
  • Kauai reaches 82% and reopens schools — One week after Hurricane Lowell, the island utility had restored about 82% of its members by Tuesday, with the restoration workforce grown to roughly 250 as mainland and Hawaiian Electric crews joined the co-op. Schools have begun reopening and most of the island is back at work. Crews have isolated the Princeville–Wainiha transmission section to repair it while energising around it; the North Shore from Moloaa to Haena and remote areas including Kokee remain dark, with one to two weeks still estimated for the most isolated customers. (Hawaii News Now, Honolulu Star-Advertiser, The Garden Island)

🔋 Electric Vehicles
  • The Kia PV7 gets real numbers — Kia’s larger electric van made its production debut at IAA Transportation in Hanover on Monday with specifications attached at last: up to 5.3m long and 1.99m tall — 70cm longer, 10cm wider and 7cm taller than the PV5 — on the same E-GMP.S platform, with more than 460km of range and a 10–80% fast charge in about 25 minutes at 350kW. It launches front-wheel drive with up to 272bhp, and goes on sale in Korea and Europe in 2027, aimed squarely at the E-Transit Custom and the Trafic. (autoevolution, CleanTechnica)

It is the first of five commercial vehicles Renault plans by 2028. (electrive, CleanTechnica)
🎒 School
  • Needs you: chaperone training, before a trip is booked — A new California requirement means any parent or guardian who drives or chaperones and could be alone with students — without a staff member present — must complete Mandated Reporter Training every year. It is a free, one-hour, self-paced online course, but it is gated behind the Annual Volunteer, Chaperone and Driver Application in PowerSchool: finish that first and the registration link arrives within a week. The school is asking families to do it now rather than when a trip is imminent, warning plainly that field trips get postponed or cancelled when there are not enough trained drivers.
