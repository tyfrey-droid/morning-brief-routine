🌍 World
  • Trump rejects Iran’s seven-day plan — Trump said Saturday he had turned down Tehran’s written proposal, the one delivered after last week’s New York talks: Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restart nuclear negotiations within seven days if the US lifted its naval blockade. UN Ambassador Mike Waltz said the problem was sequencing and price — Tehran wanted sanctions relief and access to billions in frozen assets first, “asking for everything up front, with a promise that they would then talk.” Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran will not soften its terms “even if it comes to a doomsday war,” and Iran’s UN delegation told reporters it had no plans to meet the Americans again this week. (CBS News, NPR, The Times of Israel)
  • Five arrested near a US bomber base in England — Gloucestershire police were called just after midnight Sunday about three vans heading toward RAF Fairford, and arrested five men under the Terrorism Act and the Explosive Substances Act on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act. About 85 households were evacuated while a bomb-disposal robot worked between the vehicles in woods near the base. Fairford has been the hub for American B-1 and B-52 operations against Iran since February; counter-terror officers have said a link to Iran is one line of inquiry, and none has been confirmed. (NPR, CBS News, CNN)
  • Bangkok declares all 50 districts a disaster zone — Nearly 300mm of rain in 48 hours put water over main roads at 37 locations and forced thousands from their homes; the city opened more than 200 shelters and declared a flood emergency Saturday. The wider toll across 25 provinces is above half a million people affected and at least eight dead. City authorities say draining will take two to three days once the rain stops. (CNN, NBC News, PBS NewsHour)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • A nor’easter floods the coast from Delaware to Long Island — The slow-moving storm sat off the Mid-Atlantic through Saturday, gusting to 81 mph at Montauk Point and pushing tidal gauges to their highest marks since Sandy — 3.26 feet above normally dry ground at Atlantic City. A New York City Housing Authority employee, 56, was killed by a falling tree in Brooklyn, where four others were hospitalized in a separate tree strike. More than 130,000 customers were still without power Sunday morning, most of them on Long Island. (The Washington Post, NBC News, CNBC)
  • $810 million canceled by pocket rescission — The White House announced Friday night, days before the fiscal year closes, that it is canceling $810 million Congress had already appropriated. The largest piece is $567 million from the account funding HHS refugee resettlement, which the administration says is surplus now that border crossings have fallen; the rest includes $70 million in foreign student and faculty exchange grants, $28 million in health research it calls duplicative, and $25 million in migrant-student education programs. The mechanism is a “pocket rescission” — running out the clock so unspent funds lapse — which the Government Accountability Office and appropriators in both parties say exceeds the president’s authority. (CNBC, NBC News, Roll Call)
  • College sports bill reaches the Senate floor’s end — The Protect College Sports Act, from Ted Cruz and Maria Cantwell, cleared its last procedural hurdle 74–24 and a final vote could come this week. It would write name-image-likeness pay into federal law, replace the state-by-state patchwork, set a $48.8 million school revenue-sharing ceiling, guarantee a fifth year of eligibility while limiting transfers to one, cap agent fees at 5% and grant the NCAA and conferences a conditional antitrust exemption for enforcing those rules. All four Black Democrats in the Senate voted against advancing it, and the NAACP opposes it on the grounds that it strips athletes of bargaining rights. (ESPN, The Hill, Senate Commerce Committee)
⚡ Tech & AI
  • Four ways Apple might rebuild Vision Pro — Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman reports Apple has four concepts in development for a revamped headset, internally N224, including one that moves all the compute and battery inside the frame the way Meta’s new glasses do. Engineers are testing plastic, titanium and other materials to cut weight and cost. The caveats are large: Gurman says the work is on “life support” while Apple pushes toward smart glasses in late 2027, nothing would ship before late 2028 or early 2029, and there is no guarantee a new headset ever launches. (MacRumors, The Apple Post)
  • Mercedes signs Wayve’s AI driver into production — The two companies signed a definitive production agreement to put Wayve’s system into Mercedes vehicles within two years, following Mercedes’ investment in Wayve’s Series D in February. The technical bet is the interesting part: Wayve’s driver runs on onboard compute and sensors with no high-definition maps and no location-specific engineering, so it is meant to work on streets nobody pre-scanned. Mercedes has not named a model, a market, a launch date or an automation level. (Motor1, Wayve, TechCrunch)
  • Meta and YouTube restore the Musk documentary ads — Both platforms had refused paid promotion for the trailer to Alex Gibney’s Musk, flagging it as political content; Meta had already rejected distributor Bleecker Street’s appeal before reversing. “This was an error and the ads are being restored,” a Meta spokesperson said Saturday, describing a temporary restriction cleared on review. TikTok and X are still declining the trailer. It is a small case with a general lesson about how automated political-content rules land on documentary marketing. (TechCrunch, The Hollywood Reporter)

🌊 San Diego
  • SDPD now says why it searches plate readers — mostly — The department has logged 114,692 automated license plate reader searches since January, and since the start of the year has required a reason and a case number for each one. In 3,962 of them the reason selected was “other,” the 11th most common category. Officers must write a fuller explanation when they pick it, but the department does not publish those explanations, so the disclosure stops short exactly where it would be most useful. The city’s civilian Privacy Advisory Board recommended last November that the Council drop Flock entirely. (Times of San Diego, KPBS)
  • La Playa Taco Shop closes after 30 years — The Mission Beach shop served its last customers Sept. 19. Owner Victor Rodriguez Ocampo Sr., 54, was detained by ICE outside his National City home on Halloween morning 2025, held at Otay Mesa and deported to Mexico in January. His family kept the business running in his absence; his son Victor Jr. has since joined the Navy, and there was nobody left to run it. A fundraiser for his legal costs has taken in about $25,500. (Times of San Diego, SanDiegoVille)
