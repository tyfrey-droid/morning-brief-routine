🌍 World
  • US and Iranian envoys talk for three hours — Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi at UN headquarters in New York on Tuesday afternoon, with Qatar’s prime minister mediating for most of the session. Trump called it “very productive” and said another meeting is expected soon. Iran’s stated conditions are the immediate lifting of the US naval blockade, release of frozen Iranian assets, and an end to the war; a senior Iranian official separately told Reuters that Tehran could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if Washington eases military pressure. It came hours after Trump told the General Assembly he faces a choice between a deal and moving to “annihilate” the Islamic Republic. (Al Jazeera, Axios, CNBC)
  • An Arctic deal signed over Greenland — Trump signed a trilateral security agreement with Denmark and Greenland at the UN on Tuesday, giving the US permanent basing rights and broad access to the island’s airspace and territory, and barring non-NATO countries from establishing bases or sensitive investments there without the parties’ agreement. The text reaffirms Greenland’s sovereignty within the Kingdom of Denmark, has no expiration date, and would survive Greenlandic independence. It follows months of Trump statements about taking the territory outright, which this agreement does not do. (CBS News, Al Jazeera, CNBC)
  • Zelenskyy floats an energy-only ceasefire — After roughly 40 minutes with Trump on the sidelines of the General Assembly, Ukraine’s president said Kyiv is ready for a mutual halt to strikes on energy infrastructure — “if they will not attack our energy system, our electricity, heating system, water supplies” — and asked Trump to convene a three-way meeting with Putin “as quickly as possible.” He was explicit that a unilateral Ukrainian pause on strikes against Russian refineries was not discussed. Trump is expected to carry the proposal to Moscow. (Al Jazeera, CNBC, Kyiv Independent)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • Three news outlets sue over the White House ban — CNN, MS NOW and Politico sued the administration this week after their reporters were turned away from the White House grounds and had credentials deactivated, arguing the ban is viewpoint discrimination barred by the First Amendment. The Justice Department defended it, saying the outlets’ reporting jeopardized national security. The White House press pool has suspended its televised coverage of the president after CNN was pulled from assigned pool duty, and the White House Correspondents’ Association has called for access to be restored; the administration has meanwhile launched its own 24/7 YouTube channel. (NPR, PBS NewsHour, NBC News)
⚡ Tech & AI
  • Two frontier models ship 90 minutes apart — Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on Tuesday at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens, a 20% cut from its predecessor with cache reads down 60%; OpenAI followed the same afternoon with GPT-6 Sol and Luna at roughly half the previous tier’s API price, which it attributes to caching and inference improvements rather than a smaller model. Sol is pitched at coding and complex work, Luna at high-volume clerical tasks. The pattern worth noting for anyone paying for this stuff: the competition is now running on price and error rates rather than raw capability claims. (TechCrunch, MacRumors, 9to5Mac)
  • The new Macs went on sale Tuesday — Apple’s updated Mac mini and Mac Studio reached stores and the online store Tuesday in 30 countries, separate from the September 9 iPhone event. The mini comes with M6 or M5 Pro and starts at $899; the Studio takes M5 Max or M5 Ultra and starts at $2,499, though 512GB-memory configurations slip to late October. Apple claims the M6 mini is 40% faster on CPU and twice as fast on graphics and storage as the model it replaces. (Apple, Macworld)

He took roughly $11 million less than his maximum and waived a $7.5 million trade kicker to make the move work, and is now under contract in Toronto for three years and $165 million. It snapped a five-game Padres winning streak and a stretch of 13 wins in 14 games. The magic number holds; the next chance comes Wednesday night in the same building. (ESPN, MLB.com)
🌊 San Diego
  • A statewide emergency ahead of El Niño — Governor Newsom proclaimed a state of emergency for all of California on Monday to pre-position flood-fighting resources before winter, with forecasters saying the developing El Niño is already breaking records and could become the strongest measured. The proclamation directs state agencies to stage sandbags, pumps and equipment and to lead flood-risk reduction work; Cal OES is coordinating with San Diego County’s emergency office. The city’s sandbag program is open now, at up to 10 bags per household, filled by the resident. Laguna Beach, Dana Point and Carlsbad declared local emergencies last week over coastal erosion. (CNN, ABC 10News, Governor of California)
🌺 Kauai
  • Under a thousand still without power — Kaua‘i Island Utility Cooperative passed 98% restored on Monday, two weeks after Hurricane Lowell, with the outage count falling below 1,000 members for the first time. Roughly 500 of those remaining are in Wainiha and Hā‘ena, where crews are still clearing vegetation and repairing equipment, with helicopter support expected. The utility’s current estimate is that restoration work in those two communities could begin toward the end of this week. (Kauai Now, The Garden Island)

It is a passenger-configured van rather than a cargo conversion, which is the rarer thing in this segment. No US availability has been announced. (Electrek)

The demonstration is set for its Ningbo safety centre. As ever with these numbers, the charger is only half the equation — almost nothing on sale can accept that rate. (Electrek)
