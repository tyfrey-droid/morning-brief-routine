🌍 World
  • Saudi Arabia shuts its bypass pipeline — Drones launched from Iraq struck the Riyadh and Madinah regions on Thursday, injuring several people and damaging the East–West crude pipeline; the Saudi energy ministry suspended the line late Friday as a precaution. The 7-million-barrel-a-day artery runs from the oil fields on the Gulf coast to Red Sea export terminals, and it is the main way Riyadh has kept crude moving without passing through the Strait of Hormuz. Brent rose about 3.5% to roughly $108 a barrel and WTI to about $103.50; Riyadh has not said how badly the line is damaged or when it reopens. (CNBC, Al Jazeera, The National)
  • A ferry capsizes in the Java Sea — The Virgo Transport 8 was carrying more than 240 people from Surabaya, on Java, to Banjarmasin on Borneo when its captain radioed that the vessel was listing in three-metre seas early Sunday. Contact was lost around 2 a.m. and the ship overturned in the Masalembo waters, about 148km from Banjarmasin. At least 108 people have been rescued and six bodies recovered; Indonesian authorities widened the search Monday for 129 still missing. One survivor said the ferry was overcrowded, with passengers sleeping in hallways because the cabins were full. (NPR, Al Jazeera, ABC News)
  • BRICS meets in New Delhi — Narendra Modi hosted Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping, Iran’s Masoud Pezeshkian and the rest of the eleven-member bloc at Bharat Mandapam on Friday and Saturday, under India’s chairship. It was Xi’s first visit to India in nearly seven years. The closed plenary produced a New Delhi Declaration adopted by consensus, covering reform of global institutions and economic cooperation; US tariffs, sanctions and the Iran war dominated the agenda and tested how much the members actually agree on. (CNBC, NPR)

🧬 Mito Research
  • A single-cell tool finds off-target mtDNA edits — Researchers reporting in PNAS built scMPCDS, a platform that measures mtDNA mutations and heteroplasmy in the same individual cell as its membrane potential and reactive-oxygen output — genotype and function together, rather than one then the other. Applied to DdCBE base editing, the approach most often discussed for lowering a pathogenic heteroplasmy load, it found cell-specific off-target mutations elsewhere in the mitochondrial genome that tracked with measurable changes in how those cells performed. That is the finding that matters for a family watching the editing field: bulk sequencing averages across thousands of cells and would report a clean result while individual cells diverged. This is laboratory work in cell lines and tumour tissue, with no near-term clinical implication — but it is the kind of measurement that has to exist before mtDNA editing can be assessed honestly in patients. (PNAS, PubMed, doi.org)
