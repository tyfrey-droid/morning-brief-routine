🌍 World

  • Israel's election ends in deadlock — Tuesday's vote produced no clear winner. Exit polls from the three main Israeli networks put Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud-led bloc at roughly 50 to 52 seats against about 52 for challenger Gadi Eisenkot's camp — both far short of the 61 needed in the 120-seat Knesset, and a collapse from the 68 Netanyahu's coalition held going in. Likud remains the largest single party. Arab-led parties, aligned with neither bloc, took about 18 seats, and Eisenkot has all but ruled out governing with them, which narrows his own path. Official results are not published until November 4. (CNN, NBC News, CNBC)
  • Cyclone Arnab comes ashore in India — The India Meteorological Department classified Arnab a “very severe cyclonic storm” — its third-highest category — before it made landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast on Wednesday morning with winds near 125 to 135 kph and gusts to 150. More than 180,000 people were moved out of low-lying areas of Andhra Pradesh and Odisha beforehand. Three airports suspended operations and trains and flights were cancelled. Reuters reported no loss of life in either state; an NDRF official told Al Jazeera three people had died. The centre has now moved inland. (France 24, NBC News, Al Jazeera)
  • Congo's Ebola outbreak passes 10,000 cases — The Democratic Republic of Congo's health ministry put the count at 10,060 confirmed cases and 4,887 deaths on Tuesday, making this the second-largest Ebola outbreak ever recorded and the fastest-growing in the country's history. Fifty to eighty new cases are confirmed daily across eight provinces, with Ituri and North Kivu worst hit. The strain is Bundibugyo, for which no vaccine or treatment is approved; the WHO has begun a first clinical trial of a candidate vaccine. Africa CDC says roughly 80 per cent of new cases fall outside the contact chains health workers are tracking, which is the central problem with containment. (Al Jazeera, ABC News Australia, World Health Organization)
