🌍 World
  • Trump halts planned Iran strikes — President Trump said late Saturday he was calling off a planned large-scale strike on Iran's energy infrastructure, claiming the "perimeters" of a deal had been reached that would fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end Iran's nuclear threat — contingent on an agreement being concluded "rapidly." Officials said most have since returned to Morocco and announced a new ~500-meter floating sea barrier extending the border fence. (NPR, PBS NewsHour)
  • Deadly Kyiv barrage exposes gap — An overnight Russian barrage of ballistic missiles and roughly 185 drones struck Kyiv early Saturday, killing at least nine people and wounding more than 30. Zelenskyy said only one of 27 ballistic missiles was intercepted because Ukraine has run critically short of Patriot interceptors, and pressed Washington for the systems and licenses to build its own. (NPR, Bloomberg)

⚡ Tech & AI
  • Apple debuts Klarna-run leasing — Apple launched Apple Upgrade in the US, a device-leasing program run by Klarna covering iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch, with iPhone leases starting around $18/month over 12- to 36-month terms and the option to upgrade, buy out, or return at term-end. It replaces Apple's in-house iPhone Upgrade Program, with billing handled in the Klarna app. (Apple, MacRumors, TechCrunch)
  • Apple sued over fake wallet app — Three customers sued Apple in California federal court after losing a combined $1.8 million to a counterfeit Sparrow Wallet app on the App Store, alleging Apple kept the app live — and even featured it in curated crypto collections — after an earlier theft was reported. Apple says it has since removed the impersonating apps and terminated the developer accounts. (9to5Mac, CoinDesk)
  • EU AI Act transparency rules begin — As of August 2, the European Commission's AI Office and national authorities begin enforcing the AI Act's transparency obligations: chatbots must disclose they are AI, and AI-generated or altered content, including deepfakes, must be labeled and carry machine-readable marks. Violations can draw fines up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover. (European Commission)
  • Google scraps standalone AI Studio app — Google canceled its planned AI Studio mobile app for iOS and Android despite roughly 800,000 preorders, saying it will instead fold app-building features directly into the Gemini app on mobile and desktop. The AI Studio website remains available for desktop users. (9to5Google)
  • Researchers flag AI-run cyberattack — Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 detailed a China-based actor who wired the DeepSeek model into an open-source agent framework, directing it via Telegram to autonomously scan and attack roughly 460 internet-facing systems from a single prompt. The operation was exposed when the agent published its own config and keys; researchers noted rival models had refused similar requests. (BleepingComputer, The Hacker News)

✨ One Good Thing
  • AI reveals bursts of bird evolution — University of Michigan researchers used a new AI tool, "Skelevision," to assemble more than 170,000 skeletal measurements across 2,000-plus songbird species, and found these birds evolved not at a steady creep but in rapid bursts that repeatedly lined up with major shifts in Earth's climate. The work, published in Nature Ecology & Evolution, reshapes how scientists understand adaptive radiation. (University of Michigan)
