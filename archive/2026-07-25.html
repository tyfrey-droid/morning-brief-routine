🌍 World
  • Bangladesh president resigns — President Mohammed Shahabuddin, 76, stepped down Friday roughly two years before his term ended, publicly citing health problems. Reuters reported the real driver was pressure from Prime Minister Tarique Rahman's government over Shahabuddin's long ties to ousted premier Sheikh Hasina, who has said she intends to return in December to face a court ruling. Parliament's speaker becomes acting president, with a successor to be elected within 90 days. (Al Jazeera, Washington Post)
  • Indian activist ends 26-day fast — Climate and education activist Sonam Wangchuk ended a 26-day hunger strike in New Delhi on Friday, in the presence of two federal ministers and after appeals from dozens of MPs, saying he acted "in view of possible violence in the country." His fast helped galvanize India's youth-led protest movement demanding the education minister resign over exam-paper leaks; organizers vowed to keep protesting. (NPR, The Star)
  • Iran war escalates, oil tops $100 — After a 13th consecutive night of U.S. strikes on Iran, President Trump told Axios he is "considering a massive attack," while Iran-backed Houthis struck two Saudi tankers in the Bab el-Mandeb strait — pushing oil above $100 a barrel for the first time since May. Iran also claimed retaliatory drone strikes on U.S. bases in Bahrain and Jordan. (CNN, NPR, The Irish Times)
🇺🇸 US & Politics
  • House renames Pentagon "Department of War" — The House narrowly passed (216–212) a roughly $1.15 trillion defense authorization bill that adopts Trump's request to redesignate the Department of Defense as the "Department of War" and grants troops a 5–7% pay raise; six Democrats crossed over and seven Republicans opposed. In a separate 216–214 vote, the House adopted a $95 billion budget resolution to help fund the Iran war over unified Democratic objections. (PBS NewsHour, Washington Post, Stars and Stripes)
  • Jobless claims lowest since 1969 — U.S. initial unemployment claims fell 22,000 to 187,000 for the week ending July 18 — the fewest since September 1969 and well under the roughly 215,000 forecast — signaling layoffs remain historically low even amid other economic strains. Relative to today's far larger labor force, economists note it is effectively a record low. (CNN, Bloomberg, Axios)
⚡ Tech & AI
  • Google maps how work uses AI — Google published ATLAS, an analysis of about 15 million de-identified Gemini interactions across 800 occupations and 4,000 tasks. It found AI is now used in roughly 68% of occupations but for only about 21% of a given worker's tasks — mostly as a collaborator for research, drafting and troubleshooting rather than to automate whole jobs. (Google, Axios)
  • Alphabet lifts AI spending to $205B — Alphabet beat second-quarter estimates — revenue up 24% to $119.8 billion, with Cloud up 82% to $24.8 billion — but raised its full-year capital-spending guidance to $195–205 billion to build out AI infrastructure. Shares slipped after hours as investors weighed the outlay against near-term margins. (CNBC)
  • Black Forest Labs launches FLUX 3 — The German lab behind FLUX unveiled FLUX 3, a multimodal model that generates images, video and audio — up to 20-second clips with synced native sound — from a single set of weights. It is rolling out first as gated early access for video, with image generation and an open-weight "Dev" backbone to follow. (VentureBeat)
  • WhatsApp revamps CarPlay and iPad — Meta's WhatsApp rolled out a reworked CarPlay and Android Auto experience that finally lets you hear and reply to messages hands-free, plus direct iPad sign-ups with no phone required, in-chat PDF editing via Adobe Acrobat, and sharing songs from Apple Music or Spotify to Status. (MacRumors, TechCrunch)
  • Claude Code adds iOS Simulator testing — Anthropic added native iOS Simulator support to the Claude Code Mac app in public beta, letting developers have the tool build, run and visually test iPhone apps live across up to four simulator panes without configuring Accessibility or Screen Recording permissions. It requires Xcode and works only in local sessions. (9to5Mac, MacRumors)

🧬 Mito Research
  • OMT-28 cleared to enter Phase 3 — OMEICOS Therapeutics said it had a positive End-of-Phase-2 meeting with the FDA for OMT-28 in primary mitochondrial disease, a category that includes MELAS, with the agency supporting a move directly into a pivotal Phase 3 trial. The company points to its completed open-label Phase 2a study, which it says showed improved physical condition tied to recovered mitochondrial function in responders, with a clean safety profile across 190-plus people dosed. Why it matters: it is one of the few mitochondrial-disease drug candidates advancing toward a registration trial. ⚠️ Industry-funded company announcement; the Phase 2a was open-label with no placebo control, so efficacy claims remain preliminary. (BioSpace)
