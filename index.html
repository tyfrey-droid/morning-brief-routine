  • Trump moves to end $4bn a year in aid to Israel — Trump said the United States will stop providing Israel with roughly $4 billion in annual military aid at the end of 2026, telling Fox News that Netanyahu proposed it and that Israel no longer needs the money. The mechanics are unsettled: the current memorandum of understanding runs to September 2028, Israel's Finance Ministry said it was caught off guard, and pro-Israel members of Congress have responded by writing deeper US–Israeli military integration into the annual defense authorization bill. 🌊 San Diego

  • District 5 is headed for a special election — Councilmember Marni von Wilpert won the 48th Congressional District race and is expected to resign her council seat in the coming weeks, which under the city charter triggers a special election within 90 days of the vacancy. There is no regular county or state election in that window to fold it into, so it runs on its own; Council President Joe LaCava put the cost at upward of $1 million and said the district — Scripps Ranch, Rancho Peñasquitos and Rancho Bernardo — will have no voting member until it is filled. (KPBS, CBS 8)
  • Leads widen on the county tax and reform measures — Wednesday's brief had Measure B, the countywide half-cent sales tax, sitting at roughly 50.3 per cent and too close to call. Later tallies have moved the margins out for it and for the county governance-reform measures on the same ballot. Results stay unofficial until the canvass is certified, and the Registrar has until December 3. (Voice of San Diego)
  • A veterinary sedative turns up in the local drug supply — The county's Overdose Surveillance and Response Program has issued a health advisory after medetomidine, a veterinary sedative, was detected in San Diego County's illicit drug supply, usually mixed into fentanyl. It causes deep sedation, slow heart rate and low blood pressure, naloxone does not reverse it, and stopping after regular use can bring a severe withdrawal. The Medical Examiner has recorded no medetomidine-related deaths locally so far, and the county says it cannot yet gauge how widely it is circulating; test strips are available through its harm-reduction program. (County of San Diego, CDC)
