  • A twelve-hour cache for always-on agents — OpenAI added an optional 12-hour prompt cache to its API, alongside the existing 30-minute default, aimed at agents that reuse the same instructions and tools across a whole working day. Writing to the longer cache costs 2.5 times the uncached input rate against 1.25 for the short one, while cached reads cost the same either way — so it pays only when the same context is genuinely reused many times. Developers set it per request and can mix the two, keeping shared instructions for twelve hours and recent conversation at thirty minutes. It applies to GPT-5.6 and later in the Responses API. (OpenAI)

🧬 Mito Research

  • NAD⁺ boosters fail a cell test that purines pass — A study in Mitochondrion (October 8) took skin fibroblasts from patients with Complex I deficiency and forced them onto oxidative phosphorylation by swapping glucose for galactose, a standard way to make a mitochondrial defect bite. Supplementing with the NAD⁺ precursors most sold to mitochondrial-disease families — nicotinamide riboside, nicotinamide mononucleotide, nicotinamide and trigonelline — produced no improvement in cell survival; niacin helped partially. What did work was adenosine monophosphate, and then adenosine and guanosine, and the metabolic profiling explains why: the OXPHOS-reliant condition was starving the cells of inosine monophosphate, a purine building block, and the nucleosides restored it. The authors propose purine metabolism as a target that has not been on the list. Caveats that matter here: this is cells in a dish, not a patient; it is Complex I deficiency rather than m.3243A>G; and the lead authors are at Khondrion, a mitochondrial drug developer, so read it as ⚠️ industry-affiliated. The useful takeaway for a family is the negative result, which is cheaper to act on than the positive one. (Mitochondrion, via PubMed · doi.org/10.1016/j.mito.2026.102223)
