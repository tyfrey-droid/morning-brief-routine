🧬 Mito Research
  • A way to see heteroplasmy and cell health together — A team publishing in PNAS on Monday described scMPCDS, a method that reads a single cell’s mtDNA mutations and heteroplasmy level at the same time as that cell’s membrane potential and reactive-oxygen output. Until now those were separate assays, so nobody could say which individual cells with a given mutation load were actually failing. Two results matter for MELAS. First, it makes the genotype-to-function question answerable cell by cell rather than as a tissue average — the missing link in judging whether any heteroplasmy-lowering therapy is helping the cells that need it. Second, applying it to DdCBE mtDNA base editing showed the editor produces off-target mutations that differ from cell to cell, each with its own functional consequence — a real safety finding for the editing approaches now moving toward the clinic, and an argument for screening them this way first. Still a laboratory tool, tested in HeLa and kidney-tumour tissue, not a treatment. (PubMed · DOI)
  • Registry data on metformin in m.3243A>G — The GENOMIT registry reported in EBioMedicine on Tuesday on 2,399 genetically confirmed mitochondrial disease patients across German, Austrian and Italian centres, 281 of whom had diabetes. Risk turned out to be almost entirely genotype-driven: m.3243A>G carriers — the same variant behind MELAS — had a 49% diabetes rate and a tenfold increased risk, while single and multiple mtDNA deletions and LHON variants showed no significant association, and neither did sex, BMI, smoking or blood pressure. The clinically useful part is the metformin question. Metformin is standard for type 2 diabetes but appears on caution lists for mitochondrial patients, and the registry found it was the most common non-insulin drug used, discontinued in 8 of 50 users, with neurological events reported in the literature but also long uneventful use. The authors’ conclusion is that the data do not justify avoiding it outright. Onset in these patients clustered around age 48, so this is a question for later rather than now — but it is a specific answer where there was a blanket warning. (PubMed · DOI)
