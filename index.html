🌍 World

  • COP31 collapses over fossil fuels — The UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey closed on Sunday two days behind schedule — the longest COP on record — with no overarching agreement. The proposed “Antalya Pact” failed after the EU, Norway, Belize, the Marshall Islands, Panama and Vanuatu objected that it did not address moving away from fossil fuels. Procedural decisions on adaptation, climate finance and carbon markets did pass, along with a pledge by more than 90 countries to accelerate electrification; initiatives on oceans, methane and COP reform were dropped. Secretary-General António Guterres said he was “deeply disappointed” but that the result does not change the science. (CNN, UN News, Carbon Brief)
  • A US jury convicts Huawei — A federal jury in Brooklyn found Huawei and three of its subsidiaries guilty on 10 of 12 counts on Tuesday, including bank fraud, money laundering and conspiracy to steal trade secrets. Jurors acquitted the company of the most serious charge, racketeering conspiracy, and of conspiracy to obstruct justice. Huawei’s lawyer said it will appeal and called the charges without legal or factual merit; sentencing is set for September 2027. The verdict does not change the company’s existing US export restrictions. (Al Jazeera, CNBC)
  • Iran says more tankers hit mines in Hormuz — The IRGC Navy said oil tankers struck naval mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz while using what it called an unapproved route, and warned that vessels must coordinate passage with Tehran. Accounts conflict on the number of ships — two in Wednesday’s statements, three in an earlier one — and every detail so far comes from the IRGC and Iranian state media rather than independent verification; the statements did not name the vessels or describe their crews. Iran has said it will not restore normal traffic through the strait until its demands are met, and reported attacks on shipping in the area are at their highest level since the war began. 🌊 San Diego

  • La Jolla Cove heads toward a partial closure — After a year of viral sea-lion videos and a federal wildlife conviction, most of the parties in the fight over the cove have converged on closing the beach itself while leaving the water open to swimmers — likely funnelling entry through the single staircase by the lifeguard tower, possibly with a sign-in sheet. Council President Joe LaCava, who has opposed a broad closure, says that if federal enforcement by NOAA falls short he hopes a partial closure could take effect by June 2027, the start of pupping season. Activists pressing for a full, permanent closure say it is not enough; no city vote has been scheduled. (Voice of San Diego, Times of San Diego)
  • The city sues over a federal housing rule — San Diego, the state attorney general and more than a dozen other local governments and housing authorities filed suit in Massachusetts federal court on Monday against HUD’s new “Verification of Eligible Status” rule, which takes effect December 14. It would bar mixed-status households — families in which some members are ineligible for federal rental assistance — from public housing and vouchers outright rather than prorating the subsidy, reaching an estimated 7,000-plus California households and at least 15,500 nationwide. The plaintiffs argue it violates the Administrative Procedure Act and that its paperwork demands illegally sweep in eligible noncitizen tenants aged 62 and over; City Attorney Heather Ferbert said Congress has protected these families for decades and HUD cannot rewrite the law on its own. HUD Secretary Scott Turner says the rule implements the 1980 Housing and Community Development Act as written. (Times of San Diego, KPBS, NBC 7 San Diego)
