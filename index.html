🌍 World

  • Trump, Xi and Putin sign a four-point declaration — The first-ever trilateral meeting of the American, Chinese and Russian leaders took place Thursday at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of APEC, and produced a joint “declaration of peace” covering four areas: nuclear risk, energy supplies, international justice and UN reform. The three reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and agreed to open a formal dialogue on reducing nuclear risk and curbing proliferation, but set no warhead reductions and no timetable; the text also warned the International Criminal Court and similar bodies not to “abuse their jurisdiction.” Trump said afterwards that Xi agreed the Strait of Hormuz should stay open and that Iran must not obtain a nuclear weapon, neither of which the declaration spells out. China's foreign ministry called the meeting productive; PBS NewsHour and other outlets described the statement as light on specifics. (CBC, PBS NewsHour, Al Jazeera)
  • Kushner floats NATO-style guarantees for Ukraine — Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff briefed European leaders and Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Berlin on Thursday and Kushner told reporters that Russia is “comfortable” with the concept of an Article 5-like security guarantee for Ukraine, calling it a significant step from Washington's side and saying Trump is open to it if it serves US interests. Zelenskyy said the meeting produced no common understanding of what such a guarantee would actually involve, European governments made no matching offer, and a Russian official subsequently called NATO-style guarantees unacceptable — Moscow's standing position is that Ukraine must stay permanently outside NATO before guarantees are discussed. The talks have produced no breakthrough; meanwhile a third Ukrainian strike in four days halted rail traffic across the Kerch bridge. (PBS NewsHour, CNBC, Al Jazeera)
  • George Washington leaves the Middle East — A US Pacific Fleet spokesperson confirmed to USNI News on Wednesday that the carrier USS George Washington has departed US Central Command after about three months on station, crossing into the 7th Fleet area of responsibility once USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived to relieve it. The ship had operated in the Arabian Sea since August, when it took over from USS Abraham Lincoln. Two carrier strike groups remain in the region. The Navy has not said whether the George Washington is heading straight back or will make a port call on the way east. (USNI News, Task & Purpose)

🇺🇸 US & Politics

  • The Senate again rejects Iran war powers — Senators voted 48–51 on Thursday against a concurrent resolution that would have required the president to end hostilities with Iran absent a congressional authorisation for the use of military force. It was the first vote on the war since the midterms, and it picked up no new Republican support despite the losses the party took at the polls: only Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski and Rand Paul crossed over, one fewer than in June, when a similar measure passed the Senate. The House had approved the resolution 220–204 in September with seven Republicans in favour. Because it is a concurrent resolution it would not have gone to the president for signature even had it cleared both chambers. (CBS News, The Hill, Roll Call)
  • A third Supreme Court bid on Venezuelan TPS — The Justice Department filed an emergency application on Wednesday asking the justices to block a district court ruling — upheld by the Ninth Circuit — that the administration lacked authority to end the Temporary Protected Status granted to Venezuelans under Biden. It is the government's third trip to the high court on this question; the justices granted interim relief twice in 2025. The filing came ahead of a temporary order expiring today that had been holding TPS restoration in abeyance, and government lawyers told the court that roughly 1,800 Venezuelans would have to be released from detention if the justices do not act. About 600,000 people hold the status. The court has given the other side until November 30 to respond. (AP via WRAL, CBS News)

⚡ Tech & AI

  • The UK loses its secrecy fight over the Apple order — The Investigatory Powers Tribunal ruled Thursday that the Home Office cannot keep secret the existence of the technical capability notice it served on Apple, finding the government had not shown that confirming it would damage national security. The order dates to January 2025 and demanded access to encrypted iCloud data; Apple refused to build it and instead withdrew Advanced Data Protection for new UK users, and a second, narrower notice aimed only at British users followed in July 2026. The tribunal did not rule on whether the demand itself is lawful — only that Apple's challenge, and parallel cases from Privacy International and Liberty, can now largely proceed in open court. For a Mac or iPhone owner outside the UK nothing changes today, but the case is the clearest test yet of whether a government can compel a break in end-to-end encryption. (MacRumors, Computer Weekly, AppleInsider)
  • Apple heads to a Paris repair trial — Apple Inc and Apple Distribution International will face a four-day criminal trial in Paris beginning June 17, 2027, on charges of obstructing the repair and refurbishment of a range of iPhone models between November 2021 and July 2024, according to a source close to the case who spoke to AFP. The practice at issue is parts pairing, which electronically ties a component to a specific handset; the advocacy group HOP, which brought the complaint, frames it as planned obsolescence. Apple says it “strongly contests these unfounded allegations,” argues parts pairing protects privacy, security and safety, and points to its expanded support for used genuine parts since the complaint was filed. Nothing changes for repairs now, but it is the first criminal case anywhere to test parts pairing. (France 24, MacRumors)
  • Anthropic's debut is the largest IPO ever — Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday, pricing 980 million shares at $83 — above its $70–$78 range — and raising about $81.3 billion, which passes SpaceX's $75 billion June listing as the biggest initial public offering on record. The stock opened at $111.50, roughly 34% above the offer price, then gave most of that back to close at $97.17, up about 17%; CNBC put the resulting market capitalisation near $1.8 trillion, and published valuation figures vary with the measurement point. The company also raised $26 billion privately, and its seven co-founders sold close to $2 billion of stock in the offering. Asian markets fell the same day on AI-bubble worries, so the debut is being read both as a milestone and as a top-of-market signal. (CNBC, Al Jazeera, TechCrunch)

🌊 San Diego

  • A seawall section drops into the ocean — A large stretch of the seawall along Sunset Cliffs Boulevard in Point Loma collapsed into the water after heavy surf, and the city has closed part of the adjacent trail and a parking lot. KPBS reports that recent storms and swell have already left visible damage up and down the county coast before the strongest part of this winter's El Niño pattern arrives, and that the Port of San Diego is making emergency repairs to the Bayshore Bikeway in Imperial Beach.   • San Diego Unified will require e-bike permits — The school board has approved a districtwide policy requiring students who ride an e-bike to or from school to complete a safety course and carry a free district-issued permit, effective January 5. The district brought the rules forward as student e-bike injuries climbed; the proposal had been floated to trustees earlier in the week before the unanimous vote. Enforcement details — what happens to a student riding without a permit — are still being worked out at the school level. (Times of San Diego, ABC 10News)
  • Vista elects a 28-year-old mayor — Kenneth Zuniga has won Vista's mayoral race, making him the first Democratic Socialists of America-backed mayor in San Diego County; he finished just over 1,100 votes ahead of independent Joe Green, who conceded late last week. Zuniga says his priority is more affordable housing in the city. inewsource frames it as part of a broader shift in North County, where several cities elected younger and more left-leaning councils this cycle. County results are certified December 3. (KPBS, inewsource)
