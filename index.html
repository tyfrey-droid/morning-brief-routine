⚡ Tech & AI

  • WORKFLOWPro Tools puts stem separation in the free tier — Avid shipped Pro Tools 2026.11 on November 3 with an AI Stem Separator bundled into every tier, including the free Intro version, at no extra charge. It splits a mixed recording into as many as six stems — vocals, drums, bass, guitar, keys and other — and runs on your own machine, so nothing is uploaded; you drag audio into the extension, audition the stems and send them back as separate tracks. For an editor that is a practical way to pull a usable dialogue or vocal track out of location audio or an archival mix without buying a separate plug-in or round-tripping to another app. The release also introduces the Extensions plug-in format and MIDI Polyphonic Expression. The catch is that stem separation is a rescue tool, not a mix: expect artefacts on dense material. (Newsshooter, Avid, RedShark News)
  • WORKFLOWClaude can now read your accounts — Anthropic opened a Personal Finance beta on November 5 that links bank, credit card, loan and investment accounts to Claude, puts balances and spending on one page, and lets you ask questions about your own money in plain language. 🧬 Mito Research

  • A metabolic weak point above 70 per cent heteroplasmy — A study in Nature Metabolism on October 27 looked at what high mutant-mtDNA loads actually do to a cell's metabolism, and found the damage arrives in stages rather than steadily: in cells carrying matched mtDNA deletions, respiration fell in proportion to heteroplasmy, but redox imbalance only appeared above 50 per cent, and above 70 per cent the cells developed integrated stress response–linked defects in protein synthesis. The finding that matters is what reversed those defects — either supplying asparagine or raising expression of pyruvate carboxylase, the enzyme whose scarcity left the cells unable to make their own asparagine once respiration was compromised. Patient-derived tumour cells with a high-heteroplasmy complex I mutation showed the same dependency. Two honest limits: this is cell-culture work, not a treatment, and the mutations studied were large mtDNA deletions rather than a point mutation such as m.3243A>G, so whether the same asparagine bottleneck applies there is untested. What makes it worth noting is that it identifies a specific, supplementable metabolic dependency that appears only at the high heteroplasmy levels where disease is severe. (Nature Metabolism, PubMed)
